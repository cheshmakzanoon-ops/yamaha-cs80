# Where the shipping eight-voice core spends CPU

On the profiled Linux x86-64 release build at 48 kHz and 64 frames, the oscillator family accounted for **42–45%** of samples in all three scenes. The exact batch filter solve accounted for about **16%** in held factory sound. When the eight factory voices received moving MPE controls, filter coefficient setup accounted for **13–15%**. These are exclusive sampled shares of standalone DSP, not savings estimates or native plugin measurements. [Six captures, workload, replay and limitations](../experiments/shipping-core-profile/README.md).

Two interventions tested those findings:

* **Reuse coefficients across different voice lanes in one low-pass pass.** The sole primary, factory-moving, measured -1.29% standalone CPU reduction (95% whole-pair interval -10.03% to +4.13%; primary floor 11.71%). Controls also had to clear their predeclared −3% lower bounds. See the [complete 256-observation screen and all four scenes](../experiments/lp-cross-lane-reuse/cost/README.md). Positive values mean less CPU; negative values mean more. Helper hits alone are not CPU evidence. [What was changed and how numerical, FP and lifecycle behavior were tested](../experiments/lp-cross-lane-reuse/README.md).
* **Avoid materializing a large oscillator endpoint copy.** Two independent revisions were rejected before cost testing. One changed the audio continuation after a floating-point trap; the other repaired that case but changed a retained public diagnostic event after a different interruption. Neither demonstrates an ordinary finite-audio regression. [Patches and executable counterexample sources](../experiments/oscillator-endpoint-copy/README.md).

The production DSP is unchanged by this research checkpoint. A hotspot percentage gives the next experiment a reason to exist; it is not evidence that eliminating that percentage is possible.

A separate [nine-scene stock oscillator census](../experiments/wsc-terminal-occupancy/README.md) found that all 262,144 late fixed four-rank groups in each eight-voice factory fixture reached an ordinary final node-evaluation request. Earlier controls and boundary events still had to run in scalar order. Only 83.49% of held groups and 11.25% of moving groups had an unchanged, uninterrupted *whole* tick. This documents a potential meeting point for four scalar tasks; it is not a prediction of vector eligibility, a timing result, or a production change.

### A real four-rank implementation, not a CPU win

[Scalar staging and the four-lane implementation](../experiments/wsc-terminal-staging/README.md) preserved the tested audio, internal state, ordered RNG draws and floating-point fault continuations. The four-lane backend actually ran in the intended eight-voice fixtures; the measured Pure control never entered it.

In the [complete, one-shot 216-observation standalone cost screen](../experiments/wsc-terminal-staging/cost/README.md), held factory stock / staged scalar / vector used **0.575 / 0.521 / 0.557 CPU seconds per audio second**. The primary vector-versus-stock estimate was **3.10% less CPU** (95% whole-round interval **1.83% to 4.37%**), below the preregistered rule's **21.48%** superiority floor from same-binary calibration. Moving factory and Pure did not meet their secondary noninferiority bounds. All observations are retained; nothing was rerun to improve the result.

The staged scalar arm's **9.45% lower** held-factory point estimate versus stock was descriptive, not the primary test. Adding the packed backend **cost 7.02% more** than that scalar arm in the same held case. These are contrasts between entire implementations, not isolated arithmetic costs. Neither experimental arm was promoted; this is not a plugin or DAW measurement. Production DSP remains unchanged.

As a separate release check, the two exported patches were applied to clean copies of the shipping commit. All 193 / 196 / 201 source files matched the tested stock / scalar / vector trees. The bundled [public-render reproducer](../experiments/wsc-terminal-staging/METHOD.md) was then run on those fresh trees. Stock twice, scalar, vector and reverse-linked vector each matched the archived 17,019,552-byte observation stream and its seven real floating-point exceptions with the same normalized sites and continuations. This validates that particular reproducer, not the cost result or unbundled test suites.

### Different scalar design: finish one rank immediately

A [separate single-rank candidate](../experiments/wsc-immediate-rank-scalar/README.md) finishes each ordinary oscillator tick where stock does, without four-rank staging or AVX. Its compiler leaves 48 of 160 endpoint bytes in registers at one location, while retaining the full persistent endpoint write and introducing an actual helper call; this is not a prediction of total memory traffic or speed. In all four intended workloads the candidate's diagnostic output matched stock. The counter observed 1,048,576 ordinary immediate-terminal completions in the intended window of each active fixture and zero in Pure. Original-route traps, subsequent audio, and both tested link orders also matched. This is a **correctness result, not a CPU measurement**; no change to production DSP follows from it.

The [stock-only paired-clock qualification](../experiments/paired-stock-clock/README.md) then tested whether this VM could measure small effects reliably. All 20 pairs completed with valid observations. All eight deliberate extra-work runs detected the added work, yet 7 of 12 same-stock shams exceeded the allowed ±1.5%. The 90th percentile of paired-cycle apparent effects was 11.09%, against a 4% limit. Re-examining the *already recorded* wall interval was not a remedy: its corresponding 90th percentile was 11.76%, and the same 7 of 12 shams exceeded ±1.5% (descriptive only). No timing comparison of the new scalar candidate was run. An [independent Callgrind check](../experiments/callgrind-oscillator-context/README.md) found that its guest floating-point controls differed from the required native fixture, before any candidate instruction comparison. A stable target-hardware native measurement remains necessary to determine the candidate’s actual benefit.

### Sixteen-voice extension: redundant modulation validation, small absolute cost

The second instrument revalidates three already-normalized modulation shapes and 16 routing slots on every 16-voice callback. A [guarded prototype](../experiments/extension-modern-mirror/README.md) skips only that second validation under qualified conditions; changes to routes, retriggers, route rebuilding and transport still follow the stock sequence. In an [80-callback real Linux VST3 host test](../experiments/extension-modern-mirror/native/README.md), 474,170 captured audio, state and floating-point bytes matched stock. A separate observed module entered the new route in all 40 sixteen-voice callbacks and none of the 40 eight-voice callbacks. Both candidate modules were hybrid release prototypes using original unchanged objects, not clean production builds.

In the [one-shot isolated method test](../experiments/extension-modern-mirror/cost/README.md), **4 of 6 identical-stock controls and all 4 deliberate-extra-work controls missed their preregistered bounds**. All 80 observations are retained. The hot repeated calls read 328 versus 91 ns for the default configuration and 595 versus 284 ns for dense routing (stock versus candidate medians across the five comparison batch means). Even the unqualified median paired differences scale to just **0.18 and 0.24 milliseconds per second of audio** at 48 kHz and 64 frames, assuming the hot-loop cost applies to 750 block-boundary calls. These are not actual callback or DAW savings. There is no eight-voice benefit, and the production wrapper is unchanged.

### Eight-voice BBD: three repeated tap setups out of seven

In four authentic eight-note scenes with chorus switched off, the BBD clock and delay history still advanced. Across 197,120 seven-tap passes, taps 2, 4 and 6 (zero-based) always selected the same cursor and generated the same cubic coefficients as their immediately preceding taps. This was one shared pass feeding two downstream chains, not two passes. The BBD function itself held only 0.64–1.37% of whole-core samples in the earlier six captures, and those samples cover more than tap setup. The [stock-only cursor census](../experiments/bbd-cursor-census/README.md) is not a cache implementation or a speed forecast.
