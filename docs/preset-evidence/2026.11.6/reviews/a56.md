## What “521 changed sound controls” establishes

The A15 claim is correct with its stated definition: all **521 musical programs**
change at least one previously published parameter other than master `volume`.
It includes cumulative `.5` work. It does not mean every program received a new
filter, envelope, oscillator or explicit touch-depth edit.

I additionally excluded `volume`, `output_level`, rank `mix`, both `vcf_level`
controls, both `v_level` controls and both rank pans. **493** musical programs
still have changed controls. **28** then have no remaining direct parameter
change. Their changes are exclusively rank VCA baseline `v_level`, with optional
master-volume calibration. The 28 are:

| Family | Existing programs with only those baseline/volume changes |
|---|---|
| Hardware Studies (3) | Tone I: Funky 1; Tone II: Funky 2; Tone II: Funky 4 |
| Bass (19) | Frozen Bass 95; Analog Bass; Resonance Bass; Sine Bass; Sub Bass; Brass Bass; Octave Bass; Pulse Bass; Round Bass; Velocity Bass; Rubber Bass; Dark Bass; Bright Bass; Fifth Bass; Squelch Bass; Ring Bass; Sub Wobble Bass; Pressure Bass; Contour Bass |
| Brass (1) | Pressure Swell Brass |
| Keys (1) | Pressure Keys |
| Plucked (4) | Funky Pluck I; Funky Pluck II; Drone Pluck; Pressure Pluck |

This is deliberately conservative control-name classification, **not a finding
of 28 gain-only audio changes**. In `src/core/Engine.cpp`, `levelCv` includes
`vLevel + initLevel * touchVelocity + afterLevel * touchPressure`, followed by
the other active level routes and bounds. `v_level` is an additive baseline before
the envelope/VCA, not a scalar applied after touch. All 28 lower that baseline on
at least one nonzero-weight rank with a nonzero velocity-to-level route. Lowering
the baseline changes the relative contrast between softer and firmer playing
even when the explicit `init_level` depth is unchanged. Rank balance can also
change effectively as the two baselines change by different proportions.

The classifier's exclusion of VCF level and rank balance is also conservative:
these can change the relative mixture of different signals. Body/source mix and
effects mix remain classified as shaping because they blend different signals;
keyboard-level contours remain classified as response because they vary by key.

Of the 28, **15 enable bodies and 13 are circuit-only**. Shared body-engine changes
are separate from recipe edits and can affect enabled programs without changing
their saved body controls. Body enablement alone does not quantify that effect.
The current `.6` shared piano/wind and selected material work is not evidence of
a timbral change in every circuit-only voice. No parameter count, route check or
historical waveform difference establishes audible preference or exact acoustic
instrument identity.

## Historical normalized-audio evidence, with its actual limits

The old `.5/verification/a33-matched-bank-audio.json` is a local matched-bank
comparison at **202 parameters**, not audio from `fcc3c8e`. Its after-inventory
hash `faed6ff610bc778dc5135a94039344d73df5d76a6d85d48b2b35cc6290862193`
matches **`development/inventory-before-long-pad-headroom.json`**. It is after the
17 earlier integration level corrections, but before the later headroom changes
to **Golden Pads 66** and **Cathedral Pad**, whose sole subsequent `.5` inventory
change was master volume. Current `.5/inventory.json` has a different hash.

I checked the old report's inventory hashes against the retained inputs and its
capture-manifest hash against the retained manifest. The manifest's executable
hash matches the report. I did **not** reanalyze the old WAV samples or assert a
new executable/source binding for them.

That historical report records one matched complete-patch gesture per program:
523 addresses, 48 kHz stereo, velocity approximately 0.8, zero pressure, matched
note/hold/tail, and 2,149.028625 seconds per version. Its counts separate:

- 519 musical programs audible at zero pressure: all changed raw PCM; 516 have
  equal-RMS residual above 0.001; 188 have spectral Hellinger distance above 0.01.
- Electric Leads 136 and Pressure Lead: silent at zero pressure, with separate
  matched pressure=1 captures showing changed audio for both.
- Two Init programs: byte-identical historical PCM.

The normalized calculations are analysis only; delivered audio was not normalized.
The thresholds are descriptive and are not perceptual or quality acceptance
thresholds. Phase, tuning, articulation and effects can change the residual.

**Noise Pad, Ether Pad and Sub Bass** are accurately identified as *almost uniform
gain changes under that single 0.8-velocity gesture*, with residuals approximately
0.000361, 0.000117 and 0.000635 respectively. A33's separate soft/firm and common-
master controls document changed playing contrast: 3.095→6.915 dB,
2.650→6.821 dB and 7.455→9.381 dB. Sub Bass is one of the 28 baseline-only recipes,
illustrating why that control classification cannot be equated to gain-only
behavior across velocities. Those remain historical measurements; they do not
substitute for acceptance of the current archive or a whole-bank listening test.
