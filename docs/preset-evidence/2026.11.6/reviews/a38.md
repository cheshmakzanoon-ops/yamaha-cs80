# A38 — material-voice note lifecycle

## Scope and executed result

Exercised all nine existing material programs through the complete Engine at 48 kHz: Tubular Bells, Gong, Temple Bell, Agogo, Music Box, Banjo, Koto, Harp and Kalimba. The original factory settings are used for overlap, pedal, allocation and panic cases. A separate expression scene explicitly enables MPE and a 12-semitone member range; that scene is a control-routing exercise, not a change to the saved factory recipes.

The final probe passes **1,629 checks, zero failures**, generating **122.22 seconds**. All samples are finite and below full scale; largest absolute sample is **0.233249992132 (−12.643567 dBFS)**. No production code, factory preset, registered test gate or workflow changed.

## What the probe establishes

- Repeated same-key notes retain separate onset ownership. A wrong-channel note-off is inert; each correct note-off releases only the newest matching onset, preserving the older velocity identity.
- Channel sustain holds both released onsets on its own channel. MIDI All Notes Off (CC123) respects a still-held pedal. Pedal-up clears all sustained onset flags, and ordinary release eventually retires the physical voices.
- Sixteen simultaneously held keys do not exceed eight physical circuits. The source-performance allocator intentionally keeps the first eight held circuits rather than stealing them. Releasing one admits the lowest waiting key with zero new-strike velocity. Releasing the remaining keys leaves no held or pedal-owned voice.
- Global hard panic immediately clears the active voice count and activity mask. A new onset afterwards cannot resurrect the old waiting keys. Shared circuit and output histories are measured separately from active voices.
- With MPE enabled, pressure and member bend address only the selected same-key channel. Member All Sound Off (CC120) preserves its neighboring member. The neighbor’s ordinary CC123 release retires normally. These are routing/telemetry and output-safety checks, not a spectral pitch-accuracy measurement.

## Discriminating negative control and retained initial assumptions

The same final executable deliberately omits the first preset’s CC64 pedal-up before the observation, then performs cleanup. That run exits 1 with **exactly one failure**: “Tubular Bells: pedal-up clears every sustained onset.” All other checks pass. This distinguishes actual pedal release from a probe that merely reports eventual decay. No production mutation is involved.

Two preliminary timing assumptions were rejected and their original logs are retained. Requiring less than −60 dBFS RMS in the first quarter-second after panic failed eight cases because it included preceding audio and shared path history. Draining only twice the reported group latency plus a small frame guard still failed six cases: group delay does not bound recursive PRA/output history. The final probe records the early output and the separate later window instead of describing immediate voice deactivation as immediate digital silence. These were failures of newly authored probe assumptions, not pre-existing product gates or demonstrated production defects.
