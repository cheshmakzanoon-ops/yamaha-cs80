# A18 — all 59 Strings programs

## Finding

No production correction is indicated by this independent review. Every one of
**59 existing Strings programs** preserves its name, category and ordered host
address and changes **4–8 previous non-master controls** against the published
2026.11.4 inventory. There are **28 original-address programs and 31 curated
programs**, **49 body-enabled programs and 10 circuit-only programs**. All 59
non-master sound vectors are distinct. The appended material controls remain
neutral throughout this family. These checks cover the complete category,
including the three Modern programs and the original named variations.

The comparison is cumulative: these recipes retain the prior .5 programming;
this review does not attribute 59 additional recipe edits to .6. It also does
not equate a changed control vector with acoustic realism.

## Roles remain distinct

The retained programming reduces fixed rank levels and extends initial-touch
and pressure ranges, rather than simply turning up master volume. Solo Violin
uses a nearly complete bowed main body, an independently fading friction rank,
dry centered output and 0.024-second main attack. Solo Cello retains the lower
16-foot registration, a larger 2.1 body size, 0.045-second main attack and its own
short friction rank. Chamber Strings uses two closely tuned body sizes and
staggered entrances without chorus. Tutti retains separately voiced 16-foot and
8-foot sections. Slow Strings and Bowed Swell Strings retain long entrances
and independent ending contours.

Pizzicato uses struck nylon/steel bodies and zero amplitude sustain; Spiccato
uses a bowed spectrum with a detached amplitude envelope. Swell Strings keeps
zero initial-touch gain and expresses its main dynamic range through held
pressure. String Machine keeps its intended constant-level response. The ten
circuit-only programs include PWM, thin/hollow, string-machine and synthetic
orchestral roles; forcing these onto one bowed model would erase named
intentions. Original-bank mappings preserve the corresponding roles and their
individual finish controls. The Modern voices retain their separately authored
phaser, PWM and ensemble behavior.

**8.60 dB for Solo Cello**, **11.00 dB for Pizzicato** and **7.59 dB for

**15.39 dB** when pressure eases. String Machine's released chord is **16.49 dB**
below its held level in the tested 120–150 ms post-release window. Slow Strings
and Bowed Swell Strings bloom **17.5 dB** and **14.9 dB** above their opening
windows while retaining an audible final held body. The logs retain the exact
unrenormalized factory measurements and test definitions.

## Limits

This is authored synthesis with measured gesture and envelope behavior. No
blind listening, recorded-instrument matching, instrument-range authenticity
or native-host qualification is established here. The historical
[long-bow investigation](../../2026.11.5/reviews/a39.md) retains its five initial
release-floor failures and subsequent chorus-floor diagnosis; those outcomes
are not silently converted into passes by this review. The current tests use
their existing relative release and audible-body limits.
