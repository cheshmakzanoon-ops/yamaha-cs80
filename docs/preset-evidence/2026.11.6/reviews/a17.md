# A17 — complete Keys-family review

## Scope and identity

Reviewed all **45 Keys programs**, including the original-bank variations,
against the published 2026.11.4 inventory. Every name, category, order index
and host address is preserved. Every program changes at least one preceding
non-volume control; the range is **1–9 controls per program**. These are
cumulative changes retained from .5, not a claim that all 45 recipes were
rewritten in .6. The complete per-program accounting is in
[keys-coverage.json](../verification/a17/keys-coverage.json).

Every Keys program uses an active instrument body. Source review distinguishes
piano strings, tine and reed pickups, tangent strings, fixed-touch quills and
metal modes, with different contact, spectrum and decay controls. The original
Keys variations explicitly disable inherited portamento, initial bend and
pitch LFO terms. Pressure Keys deliberately retains a sustaining circuit bed;
Saw Pulse Piano likewise advertises its hybrid character. Those programs are
not evaluated as unprocessed acoustic pianos.

## Fresh full-family renders and regressions

The local candidate source files inspected here match committed source
`fcc3c8e055223fe4f786ca162ff46076c33471c8`. These are local core-engine results, not native plugin qualification.
Executable and inspected source SHA-256 values are recorded in
[measured-summary.json](../verification/a17/measured-summary.json).

Rendered every Keys program at MIDI 60, 48 kHz, pressure zero, velocities
0.25 and 0.85, with a 1.4-second hold and 0.6-second release tail: **90 complete
patch captures**, without output normalization. The capture helper retained
metrics rather than WAV files. All samples were finite and within full-scale
bounds; the largest sample peak was **−22.315 dBFS**. This is a single-note
family check, not a replacement for polyphonic bank-level qualification.

The 40 touch-sensitive programs have **8.160–10.969 dB** firm/soft contrast in
the helper's onset RMS window. The five quill registrations have only
**0.081–0.110 dB**, consistent with their intentionally near-flat touch law.
Those five are Harpsichord I/II, Lute Harpsichord, Bright Keys 107 and Dark
Keys 108. Flat harpsichord touch is therefore not counted as a defect.

## Remaining model limitation

**Dulcimer**, and its inherited **Distant Keys 113** variation, still uses the
Standard Steel body on both ranks. Its prose describes hammered steel strings,
but the Standard Steel implementation uses the shared pluck-position comb and
steel harmonic falloff/body response; it has no distinct hammer-contact
material. Its separate courses, independently damped partials and touch
response remain present. This is a concrete modeling simplification to retain
in the scope record, not evidence from listening that the preset is unusable.
A dedicated hammered-string excitation would need its own audible causal and
compatibility checks before being described as corrected.

No production code, preset identities, test gates or public links were changed.
No human listening evaluation, measured-instrument fit or acoustic
indistinguishability was established. Historical polyphony/pedal and matched
baseline evidence remains separately attributed in the
[.5 keyboard review](../../2026.11.5/reviews/a28.md) and
[.5 complete-bank audio review](../../2026.11.5/reviews/a33.md).
