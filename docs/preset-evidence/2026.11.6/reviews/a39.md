# A39 — complete-preset mono and stereo translation

## Scope and method

Rendered all nine new material voices plus **Analog Piano, Stage Keys, Electric Piano I, Electric Piano II and Reed Piano** at MIDI 48, 60 and 72, velocities 0.25/0.85, pressure zero and 48 kHz. Each complete two-rank capture holds for 1.4 seconds and releases for 0.8 seconds. The **84 candidate captures** are finite and below full scale; the largest peak is **−23.170614 dBFS**.

The analysis uses the unmodified stereo float WAVs and a mono fold-down of `(L + R) / 2`. It compares mono energy with mean left/right energy, channel balance, and Hann-windowed spectral energy in the dominant band and the 1–16 kHz upper band. For inharmonic materials, the dominant band is deliberately not labeled a fundamental. Onset, held-body and release windows are 0.025–0.35, 0.6–1.2 and 1.55–2.1 seconds.

To avoid ratios of effectively silent data, windows below −100 dBFS RMS and bands below −45 dB relative spectral power are excluded. These are stated analysis inclusion floors, not new production acceptance limits. **216 of 252 candidate windows qualify**: all 84 onset and 84 held windows, plus 48 release windows. Quiet releases are recorded rather than assigned misleading cancellation ratios.

## Findings

| Cohort | Maximum broadband mono loss | Maximum dominant-band loss | Maximum upper-band loss |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Nine new material voices | 0.00001451 dB | 0.00001451 dB | 0.00001455 dB |
| Analog Piano / Stage Keys | 0.00002844 dB | 0.00002844 dB | 0.00002867 dB |
| Electric Piano I / Reed Piano | 0.00014856 dB | 0.00014856 dB | 0.00015128 dB |
| Electric Piano II, with its authored chorus | 2.964142 dB | 3.980078 dB | 3.061228 dB |

The dry voices are effectively dual-mono in these captures; their very small losses reflect slight channel-gain differences, not meaningful stereo cancellation. Maximum absolute left/right balance difference across all candidate windows is **0.108293 dB**.

Electric Piano II is a separate, intentional stereo-effect case. Its largest broadband reduction occurs in the MIDI-72 soft release window; its largest dominant-band reduction occurs in the MIDI-60 soft release window. This is a real mono fold-down tradeoff, not a numerical near-silence observation: the latter band contains −16.094 dB of the window's stereo spectral power.

Six additional captures from the exact preceding `c76ad288` executable show that **all six Electric Piano II baseline/current pairs are sample-identical**. Thus this cohort's chorus mono loss is retained behavior, not introduced by the new material or piano work. No source adjustment was warranted.

Six preceding-source Analog Piano/Stage Keys captures also isolate the piano geometry comparison. Across the twelve qualified paired onset/held windows, the greatest absolute change in broadband mono loss is **1.18 × 10⁻⁹ dB**. The geometry correction therefore introduces no observable mono-translation change in this cohort.

The full assignment generated **96 WAVs / 211.2 seconds**: 84 candidate, six preceding piano and six preceding chorus captures. Three known-signal controls pass: in-phase channels retain energy, anti-phase channels produce a detected null, and a 1:0.25 channel-amplitude pair gives the analytic **1.335389 dB** fold-down loss.
