# A37 — material identity and gain-confound review

The nine material voices have signal evidence beyond an increase in output
level. The strongest causal comparisons keep the complete factory patch,
MIDI note, footage, filters, envelopes and performance settings unchanged,
then change only the two Material selectors to Standard. The measured
spectra are normalized before comparison. No production correction or change
to an existing test threshold was warranted by this review.

## What the existing tests establish

| Existing voice | Material-specific observation |
| --- | --- |
| Tubular Bells | Prominent octave nominal, weak energy at the virtual strike frequency, and several upper tube modes. |
| Gong | Multiple non-bar modes and an increasing upper-to-low-mode ratio after the initial impact. |
| Temple Bell | Shell spacing and a long aftersound distinct from the compact shell. |
| Agogo | Compact-shell upper mode and shorter relative decay. |
| Music Box | Cantilever overtone spacing replaces the celesta/bar spacing. |
| Kalimba | Supported-tine upper modes replace electric-pickup odd harmonics. |
| Banjo | A different normalized harmonic profile and quicker relative body losses than Standard Steel. |
| Koto | A different normalized harmonic profile, effort-dependent upper energy and fixed-frequency response during bends. |
| Harp | A different normalized harmonic profile and longer lower aftersound than Standard Nylon. |

These are authored signal properties, not observations from fitted instrument
recordings. [A07](a07.md) and [A09](a09.md) distinguish the sources informing
the designs from the authored coefficients. The direct-body tests use their
actual twice-host-rate sample clock for Hz projections. The complete-preset
tests use 48 kHz stereo output. Bell footage multipliers match the recipes;
the Temple/Agogo decay comparison uses different MIDI keys that produce the
same sounding base frequency. The matched Material controls retain the same
footage, so recipe register changes cannot supply their difference.

[BellMaterialPresetTests](../../../../tests/BellMaterialPresetTests.cpp)
divides each mode amplitude by the RMS of the same audio window. Its
near-frequency search accommodates small tuning differences. The gong bloom
comparison and the early/late decay ratios also cancel a constant gain.
[PluckedMaterialPresetTests](../../../../tests/PluckedMaterialPresetTests.cpp)
uses an L1 distance between normalized positive amplitude vectors. Its
Kalimba grid includes nonharmonic tine frequencies. It measures a mono
projection of the stereo mix, while its touch and decay checks use stereo
RMS. The direct [plucked test](../../../../tests/PluckedMaterialTests.cpp)
instead uses a Euclidean distance between energy-normalized amplitude
vectors. A11's broad before/after comparison uses normalized power
histograms. Those distances have different definitions and are not
interchangeable percentages or perceptual scores.

The complete-preset plucked identity test proves a difference from Standard;
by itself it does not enforce the exact assignment of every compatible
material, such as Banjo versus Koto on the Steel model. The strict
`FACTORY_MATERIALS` mapping in the [A15 comparator](../../../../scripts/compare-preset-response-2026-11-6.py)
provides that separate identity check. Repeating it on the actual committed
CI audition inventory remains a publication gate.

## Fresh bounded gain-control execution

Two small probes reuse the actual production test metrics and render only
their required MIDI-60 excerpts: **19.2 seconds** across all nine programs.
They do not rerun or recount the preceding complete suites. The final probes
pass **50 checks, zero failures**.

- All four plucked material distances retain their existing **greater than
  0.12** result. A pure 3.7-times output gain produces at most
  **0.000000006693** distance, far below that threshold. Independently scaling
  the two comparison signals changes the real distance by at most
  **0.000000003354**.
- All five bell material comparisons retain their existing **greater than
  9 dB** result. A pure 3.7-times gain produces at most **0.0000000131 dB**
  normalized-mode change. Exact binary gains of 0.125 and 8 leave every
  cross-material ratio unchanged in the measured result.
- Arbitrary 3.7-times float scaling preserves all five existing bell identity
  decisions. It can alter a nearly zero generic reference through additional
  float quantization. Music Box's roughly 131 dB ratio moves **0.079956 dB**;
  the other four move at most **0.002341 dB**.

The initial auxiliary probe incorrectly required arbitrary float scaling to
preserve every bell ratio within 0.01 dB and failed its Music Box assertion.
That source and failure log are retained. The final diagnostic separates
exact power-of-two homogeneity from arbitrary-gain decision stability. **No
production assertion or acceptance threshold was altered.** The large bell
ratios compare a real mode with almost absent energy in the generic control;
they should not be advertised as 100-plus-decibel improvements in realism.

## Confounds and remaining scope

Kalimba's broad baseline difference includes its deliberate correction from
octave-up registers to the played MIDI pitch. [A11](a11.md) explicitly records
that change, and its same-patch Material-only control holds pitch constant.
The four plucked identity tests are not independent pitch-error measurements.
Sparse spectral grids, selected mode ratios and envelope windows also do not
establish complete spectra, physical model fitting, cross-instrument listening
discrimination or acoustic indistinguishability. Gong's authored material
includes both its modal shape and its bounded excitation calibration; the
gain-control probe establishes invariance to uniform output gain, not to every
possible gain change before a nonlinear signal stage.
