# A10 — Existing natural bell and music-box recipes

Five existing programs now select their own material on both ranks: **Tubular
Bells, Music Box, Gong, Temple Bell and Agogo**. Their names, order and bank
addresses are unchanged. The other 17 entries in `Bells.inc` are unchanged;
Ambient Bells elsewhere brings the unchanged remainder of the Bells category
to 18. No presets were added. The [identity comparison](../development/a10/bell-identity.json)
records the five changed source blocks.

## Voicing decisions

| Existing program | Final material and articulation |
| --- | --- |
| Tubular Bells | Twelve tube modes, weak low modes, prominent octave nominal and an audible upper nominal group. Both ranks use 8-foot footage, so the virtual strike pitch follows the played note. The former generic low body and separately pitched upper rank are replaced by one tube tuning with a brief hard contact. |
| Music Box | The celesta model selects the five-mode music-box material. Both ranks retain 4-foot footage and use sparse cantilever resonances, including the first upper mode near 6.27 times the fundamental. The short contact no longer uses an electric-piano body. |
| Gong | Both ranks use the same 16-foot plate. Twenty irregular modes develop from a low mallet thud into delayed upper energy. A bright, long main body carries almost all the sound; the brief contact contributes 1.5 percent of the rank mix. |
| Temple Bell | A 16-foot shell with stable close mode pairs and slow beating. The contact shares the main shell pitch, replacing the unrelated fifth course and fixed ring carrier. Long modal losses and the main envelope retain the low aftersound. |
| Agogo | An eight-mode compact shell at 4-foot footage, with a hard short contact and rapidly diminishing aftersound. The material supplies the metal character without an added pulse or ring oscillator. |

All five use complete body mixes, zero ring depth/amount, small voice dispersion,
zero drift, instrument-specific filter bandwidths and separately shaped contact
envelopes. Descriptions and tags now describe these final voices. The existing
electronic bell identities retain their authored fixed carriers.

[Hibbert's tube measurements](https://www.hibberts.co.uk/tubular-bells/) support
the tubular frequency ratios and octave-nominal interpretation.
[Karjalainen, Välimäki and Esquef](https://www.dafx.de/papers/DAFX02_Karjalainen_Valimaki_Esquef_bell-like_sounds.pdf)
supports independently decaying modes and split-mode beating. Material design,
the gong's delayed-mode approximation, and the distinction between measured
ratios and authored profiles are documented in [A07](a07.md). The amplitude,
filter, contact, footage and envelope settings here are authored voicing choices.

## Calibration finding and resolution

The initial Gong failed the unchanged library floor at **−33.191 dBFS**.
Increasing only its fixed VCA level reduced soft/firm contrast below the
existing **6.5 dB** contract. A brighter main body and reduced contact mix helped,
but a linear-response VCA floor of 0.42 still produced only **6.420 dB** contrast
at MIDI note 72. A trial controller curve also failed the required level and
was rejected. These failed trials are retained in the evidence directory.

The final recipe uses a **0.30** VCA floor, main-body brightness **1.0**, contact
mix **0.015**, and the unchanged linear velocity curve. The coordinating source
change raises only the gong's explicit full-strike modal ceiling from **0.72
to 0.80**. The reference-sum normalization remains independent of the actual
strike; there is no output limiter, new parameter range, or threshold change.
`CuratedLibraryLevels.inc` was not edited for this work.

The exact `LibraryAuditTests.cpp` category phrase and eight-voice stress fixture
now pass for all five programs:

| Program | Loudest 50 ms RMS, dBFS | Phrase peak, dBFS | Eight-voice peak, dBFS |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Tubular Bells | −28.501 | −17.586 | −13.872 |
| Music Box | −22.143 | −13.928 | −7.453 |
| Gong | −29.857 | −20.512 | −15.275 |
| Temple Bell | −26.341 | −17.186 | −9.880 |
| Agogo | −27.455 | −17.387 | −13.649 |

The original acceptance window is −30 to −12 dBFS; phrase peaks must be at most
−1 dBFS and stress peaks below 0 dBFS. [Final level output](../development/a10/level-audit.log)
and the [initial failure](../development/a10/initial-level-failure.log) are retained.

## Complete Engine verification

The new `BellMaterialPresetTests.cpp` passes **127 checks**. It renders the full
Engine at 48 kHz, three registers (MIDI 48, 60, 72), and velocities 0.25/0.85.
Each material's characteristic mode is compared with the same complete recipe
using the old generic material, after dividing each mode amplitude by that
render's own RMS. Thus a common gain increase cannot satisfy the identity test.
The checks also cover finite output, headroom, soft audibility, the tube's
octave nominal, the music-box cantilever spacing, and long versus compact decay.

Final Gong touch contrast is **7.447 / 7.504 / 7.192 dB** across those registers.
Its upper/body spectral contrast develops by **13.725–14.990 dB** from the early
impact to the later window. Its 5–6 second held aftersound remains at
**−55.237 to −57.509 dBFS**. At the same sounding pitch, the temple bell retains
far more 1.5–2 second aftersound than the agogo. The existing
`MalletPresetRefinementTests.cpp` is unchanged and passes **136 checks**, including
touch checks for all 23 Bells-category programs. There is no conflict with its
previous assumptions.

Logs: [new material contracts](../development/a10/material-preset-tests.log),
[unchanged mallet regression](../development/a10/mallet-regression.log).
