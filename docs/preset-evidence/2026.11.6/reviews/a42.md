# A42 — realism and claim boundaries

The retained evidence supports a cumulative revision of **all 521 existing musical programs**, preservation of **523 ordered identities**, and specific improvements in excitation, decay, tuning and expressive behavior. It supports describing the voices as more specifically programmed and their implemented material responses as distinct. It does **not** establish that every name now denotes an acoustically indistinguishable replica, that listeners prefer every change, or that a numerical spectral difference measures perceived quality.

This review read all fourteen family reports, A05/A07/A09 and the wind correction record. Its structural accounting sums to 521 musical programs plus two Init entries. Four inspected production inputs match `fcc3c8e055223fe4f786ca162ff46076c33471c8` exactly. ## What each kind of evidence establishes

| Evidence | Supported statement | Further conclusion not established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete inventories and fourteen family reviews | Every musical program has changes in preceding non-master controls relative to published `.4`; names, categories and addresses are preserved. This includes retained `.5` work. | Every recipe was newly rewritten in `.6`, or every changed control produced a listener-preferred improvement. |
| Rendered touch, held decay, pressure, interval, timing and release checks | The tested voices exhibit the specified programmed behavior under the recorded gestures and settings. | All registers, phrases, hosts or musical contexts were assessed by ear. |
| Gain-normalized generic/material comparisons | The nine specialized materials change measured spectral shape and decay beyond a uniform gain increase. | A distance from a generic model is a distance toward one particular real instrument; very large mode ratios are perceptual quality gains. |
| Piano course and fixed-Hz response controls | The defined tuning and moving-pitch response faults are corrected relative to their matched software controls. | A complete acoustic piano, nonlinear wind instrument or measured resonant body has been reconstructed. |
| Finite output, headroom, compatibility and native-package gates | The corresponding numerical, state and delivery contracts pass when their actual results are accepted. | Passing technical gates proves timbral realism or a human listening verdict. |

The reviews deliberately retain electronic identities: synth brass, PWM strings, organ registrations, ring effects, pressure leads, pads and arpeggiated voices have their own intended gestures. Their names do not necessarily identify a unique acoustic object against which an exact match could be measured. See the [Brass](a19.md), [Organ](a24.md), [Polysynth](a26.md), [Leads](a27.md), [Sequences](a28.md) and [FX](a29.md) reviews.

The capture tool makes a single note-on, applies a fixed pressure, then releases the note. This roster therefore does not itself provide repeated-strike, polyphonic, pedal, continuously changing pressure/bend or complete arpeggiator performances. Those properties have separate rendered probes, including the [actual sequence performances in A28](a28.md). Unnormalized clips preserve real program levels; listening comparisons should keep a consistent playback setting and should not interpret a louder excerpt alone as a closer acoustic match.
