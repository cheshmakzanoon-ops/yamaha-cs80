# A07 — Modal material bodies

Six saved material specializations now give the existing bell, celesta and tine
models distinct resonator identities. The separate material selector leaves the
model and variant selectors, ranges and defaults unchanged. Unsupported pairs
fall back exactly to their standard model. Selection is captured at note onset.

| Material | Implemented distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Tubular bell | Twelve tube modes, weak lowest modes and a prominent octave nominal; the strike pitch is virtual. |
| Gong | Twenty irregular plate modes, a prompt low thud and delayed upper development. |
| Temple bell | Twelve shell modes including close, stable pairs for slowly beating aftersound. |
| Agogo | Eight compact shell modes, a hard contact and shorter material decay. |
| Music box | Five sparse cantilever modes, without celesta's free-bar spacing. |
| Kalimba | Four bridge-supported tine modes, without electric-piano pickup harmonics. |

The generator retains fixed storage and its existing bounded sample loop. All
new coefficient/profile work is performed at accepted note onset. The largest
new profile uses 20 of the existing 48 mode slots. Soft strikes use the
full-strike amplitude bound, preserving their reduced upper excitation.

## Sources and limits

[Hibbert's tube measurements](https://www.hibberts.co.uk/tubular-bells/) provide
the twelve average frequency ratios used here: the reported cents relative to
P4 are converted to ratios with P4 at twice the nominal strike pitch. The source
also reports weak lowest modes. Amplitudes and losses here are authored.

[Karjalainen, Välimäki and Esquef, DAFx 2002](https://www.dafx.de/papers/DAFX02_Karjalainen_Valimaki_Esquef_bell-like_sounds.pdf)
supports independent decaying modes and split-mode beating. Its measured
handbell is not presented as a measurement of the temple or agogo profiles.

[Fletcher, 1999, sections 13.1–13.3](https://newt.phys.unsw.edu.au/music/people/publications/Fletcher1999a.pdf)
distinguishes bell/gong geometries and describes a tam-tam's developing upper
energy. The new gong uses a bounded linear approximation with delayed mode
entrances; it does not solve nonlinear mode coupling. Gong, temple and agogo
ratios, onsets and losses are authored profiles.

[Chapman, Acoustics 2008](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/acoustics2008/data/articles/000215.pdf)
reports a 5.3–5.9 first-overtone range for the studied bridge-supported kalimba
geometry. The new kalimba uses a smooth authored register law within that range;
its higher modes and losses are authored. The music-box profile starts with
theoretical uniform cantilever ratios, rather than claiming measured comb data.
[Chapman's 2012 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22280717/)
explains why bridge-supported and simply clamped-free ratios differ. Neither
profile is a fit to a particular commercial instrument.

## Verification

The new `MaterialBodyTests.cpp` directly renders audio and measures partial
ratios, temporal development, velocity response, rate consistency, reset and
unsupported-selector neutrality. **794 checks passed** at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.
Gong upper/body contrast grows **23.57–24.54 dB** from its early window to its
later window in the tested registers. Spectral checks distinguish all six new
materials from their previous generic models independently of common gain.

The first run found that agogo's authored fundamental decay was too close to
the temple profile in low/mid registers. Its loss was increased from 1.2 to
1.8 per second, and the same original assertion then passed. The initial failed
log is retained alongside the successful run.

The existing material-variant suite passed **1,132 checks**. A separately built
`c76ad288` generator and the captured candidate compared **855 default-material
streams of 12,000 samples each** with identical sample hashes. This comparison
covers every model except piano (whose deliberately changed course tuning is a
separate work item), all five prior variant values, three registers and three
rates. The retained input record identifies the captured candidate.

These tests establish the specified signal behavior and compatibility. They do
not establish acoustic indistinguishability or a human listening assessment.
