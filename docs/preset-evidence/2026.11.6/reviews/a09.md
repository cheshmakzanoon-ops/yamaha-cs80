# A09 — distinct plucked material bodies

Implemented in `src/core/InstrumentBody.h`; no preset names, addresses, parameter
ranges, engine routing or recipes were changed by this assignment.

The new saved material controls specialize the existing Steel model for Banjo
and Koto, and Nylon for Harp. Standard material and incompatible combinations
retain the existing code path. All new spectral and loss calculations execute
at accepted note onset. Live pitch uses the existing bounded formant correction
at its control boundary; no new storage, locks, allocation or sample-loop
transcendentals were introduced.

## Authored behavior and primary sources

- **Banjo:** a broad membrane response near 650 Hz, an upper bridge cluster,
  near-bridge pluck notches and frequency-selective energy loss. Woodhouse,
  Politzer and Mansour's [measurements and sound-synthesis study](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/w37w3-6r253)
  identifies a 500–800 Hz formant, string decay patterns and body transients as
  important to the instrument they measured. These coefficients are authored;
  this implementation is not their fitted membrane model.
- **Koto:** a sharper plectrum spectrum, low body/air clusters and a broader
  upper wooden response. Coaldrake's [ICA 2019 CT-model study](https://pub.dega-akustik.de/ICA2019/data/articles/001444.pdf)
  reports the paulownia structure, movable bridges and observed 85/100 Hz low
  modes. The later [JASA article](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0002427) describes
  the 85 Hz air mode and 100 Hz first eigenmode. Upper clusters, losses and
  excitation coefficients here are original voicing choices. The Steel model
  ID is retained for saved-state compatibility; it does not claim steel koto strings.
- **Harp:** a rounded lower response, a broad finger-contact bandwidth,
  characteristic pluck-position notches and longer lower aftersound. The
  [experimental harp-plucking study by Chadefaux et al.](https://www.institut-langevin.espci.fr/biblio/2020/3/5/770/files/2012_experimentally_based_description_of_harp_plucking.pdf)
  supports the importance of finger width and plucking location. Numerical
  parameters here are authored, not fitted to that experiment.

All three preserve lower excitation energy at gentle strikes through the
existing full-strike normalization. Their body responses stay in Hz while
sounding string harmonics move, including koto bends; returning to onset pitch
restores the original response. Generic Nylon, Steel and Bass do not enter this
new correction path. Nominal authored body size is 1.0; larger existing recipe
sizes would lower these profiles further.

## Rendered verification

[PluckedMaterialTests.cpp](../../../../tests/PluckedMaterialTests.cpp) passes
**666 checks** at host rates 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz with twice-rate internal render.
It covers omitted/default/incompatible materials, sounding-register and
extreme-register safety, repeatability, touch spectra, independent decays,
live pitch and return to onset. Shape comparisons normalize 12 measured
harmonic amplitudes, so gain changes alone cannot pass.

| Material | Minimum normalized spectral distance from generic body | Direct soft/firm range | Change in two-second tail, relative to matched onset |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Banjo | 0.127697 | 4.75–5.16 dB | −24.24 to −14.48 dB |
| Koto | 0.238448 | 4.94–5.07 dB | −3.81 to −1.67 dB |
| Harp | 0.128485 | 4.73–4.75 dB | +4.14 to +6.33 dB |

The pitch-to-destination comparison's worst normalized harmonic error is
**0.437308 dB**; it includes differing onset-register decay rates. Returning
from the pitch gesture has at most **0.001713 dB** error. Disabling only the new
plucked formant correction produces **27 failures** and up to **15.377985 dB**
destination error. The root's wind-formant suite remains **957/957**.

An independent same-compiler comparison against source `c76ad288` finds
**108/108 Standard Nylon/Steel/Bass captures sample-identical** over three
sample rates, three registers, two velocities and two body sizes. Input hashes
and the reproducible probe are retained below.

The first harp design failed nine shape-discrimination checks. Its plucking
position was refined before acceptance; the original failure log is retained.
The earlier material-disabled negative control failed 72 of its 558 checks.
That earlier test predates the additional 108 pitch checks and is not labeled
as a final-test execution.

These are direct-body tests without the plugin's rank envelope, filters or
output effects. They demonstrate defined signal distinctions and compatibility;
they do not establish acoustic recording equivalence or a listening-panel result.
Preset integration and bank-level calibration remain separate work.
