# A33 — repeated strikes, residual control and reproducibility

## Finding

Accepted strikes receive a monotonically increasing `voiceCounter`; each rank's private body seed is `age * 0x9e3779b9 + rank * 0x85ebca6b`, reduced to 32 bits. Host block length does not enter the seed. The seed drives contact, pluck position and applicable excitation phases. Piano secondary-course tuning remains independent of strike seed. This is deterministic variation, not a human randomness model.

## Complete-Engine repeated-note probe

The five unchanged factory programs below each play eight MIDI-60 strikes at velocity 0.7 and 48 kHz. Each event has 0.4 seconds held, 0.15 seconds of ordinary release, then hard panic and a 0.1-second gap. Panic clears every active voice and both current/previous body instances; it deliberately preserves the engine's strike counter and circuit history. No preset parameters are modified.

The output/filter stage retains a small residual even with no active voices: maximum final-gap samples range from **2.42×10⁻⁶ to 8.75×10⁻⁶**. The test therefore clones the entire state immediately before each strike and subtracts its no-new-note continuation from the measured onset. This prevents the old release/output continuation from being counted as new-strike variation. These isolated causal differences are diagnostic signals, not separately playable presets.

Onsets use 5–100 ms after key-on. Each waveform is RMS-normalized before calculating Euclidean RMS distance; 0 means identical shape and a value of 1 means distance equal to one normalized waveform RMS. Percentages below describe that mathematical distance, **not a perception score**.

| Analog Piano | 3.828–7.290% | 3.064–5.285% |
| Electric Piano I | 2.256–10.796% | 0.0494–0.1028% |
| Banjo | 3.083–8.830% | 4.767–10.230% |
| Kalimba | 0.545–4.938% | 1.048–5.571% |
| Tubular Bells | 3.672–6.164% | 3.497–9.260% |

The complete passage is replayed in another fresh engine and repeated using block sizes 17/53/251/32/127 instead of 128. **Both comparisons are sample-identical**, across all five programs. All output is finite and unclipped; the maximum absolute sample is **0.049667**. The complete-Engine probe passes **35 checks** across fifteen eight-strike passages.

The independent direct-body control uses the factory main-body model, brightness, decay, size, variant and material, at a controlled MIDI-60 pitch. Its eight age-derived seeds alter excitation while identical seeds reproduce exactly. It passes **10 checks**. This control excludes prior output, filter, oscillator and voice history. Its much smaller electric-piano shape difference demonstrates why the larger complete-Engine difference must not all be attributed to the tine excitation. The full preset includes its live circuit and rank histories.

## Piano tuning and scope

The existing rendered course-geometry regression was also compiled with the actual rank-I seed values for accepted strike ages 1, 2 and 3. It passes **212 checks**. Its maximum late-course seed spread is **0.00003442 cents** and maximum partial-course mismatch is **0.00011454 cents**. Its separate attack-excitation check retains a minimum **5.3117%** raw relative waveform difference.

This measures the isolated piano course geometry. The complete factory preset still has its authored circuit dispersion and live pitch controls; this assignment does not assert an identical absolute late pitch for every full-engine voice. Nor do repeated-waveform differences establish acoustic fidelity or human listening approval. The electric piano's isolated normalized seed-shape variation remains small; no artificial random retuning or new production change was introduced to inflate it.

The first diagnostic attempted an absolute `1e-8` silent-gap bound and an unsupported `0.001` minimum normalized seed-shape difference. Those assumptions failed and their logs remain retained. The revised probe uses the causal continuation subtraction above and reports the small electric-piano difference explicitly. No existing production test threshold was changed.
