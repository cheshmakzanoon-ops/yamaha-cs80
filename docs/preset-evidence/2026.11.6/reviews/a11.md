# A11 — existing Harp, Koto, Banjo and Kalimba recipes

The four existing programs in `src/core/curated/Plucked.inc` now use their new
saved material in **both audible ranks**. All 16 names and their order in this
file are unchanged; the other 12 recipes are byte-for-byte unchanged. No preset,
level-table entry, acceptance threshold, or parameter was added by A11.

## What changed

- **Harp:** both ranks use the Nylon model with the Harp material at nominal
  body size 1.0. A rounded finger excitation and long lower aftersound replace
  the oversized generic body and octave glint. The quieter, brighter contact
  follows the played pitch and decays first. Its release permits arpeggio overlap.
- **Koto:** both ranks use the Steel model's Koto material at size 1.0. The
  near-bridge excitation and authored board response replace the generic string
  plus clavichord accent. A small initial pitch settle remains, with restrained
  drift and a short, quiet contact. The Steel model ID is a compatibility base,
  not a claim that koto strings are steel.
- **Banjo:** both ranks use the Steel model's Banjo material at size 1.0. The
  membrane and bridge response determines the body and losses. Synthetic noise,
  the borrowed clavichord accent and the residual oscillator contribution were
  removed. The body is allowed a finite ringing tail rather than being clipped
  to the previous extremely short decay; its upper energy still clears rapidly.
- **Kalimba:** both ranks use the supported-tine material on the Tine model.
  This replaces the electric-piano pickup spectrum and celesta accent. The
  contact is quiet and short, with a longer lower aftersound. Both ranks now
  play the MIDI pitch: the previous recipe used an octave-up primary rank and
  a double-octave secondary rank. This is an intentional register change and
  should be considered when comparing an existing arrangement.

The main body contribution is 100% in each of these patches, so a residual
oscillator cannot sustain a generic buzz behind the authored decay. The second
rank is also the appropriate material, with a shorter decay and only 10–13%
rank mix. Small dispersion and drift remain; they are not used as substitutes
for instrument-specific excitation and losses.

The material research, coefficient choices and measured-generator limits are
in [A09](a09.md) and [A07](a07.md). Primary references include
[Woodhouse, Politzer and Mansour's banjo measurements](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/w37w3-6r253),
[Coaldrake's koto study](https://pub.dega-akustik.de/ICA2019/data/articles/001444.pdf),
[Chadefaux et al. on harp plucking](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3651246), and
[Chapman's supported-kalimba-tine study](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/acoustics2008/data/articles/000215.pdf).
These are authored synthesis profiles informed by those findings, not fitted
replicas of the studied physical instruments.

## Full-patch verification

[PluckedMaterialPresetTests.cpp](../../../../tests/PluckedMaterialPresetTests.cpp)
passes **124 checks** using the complete Engine at 48 kHz, MIDI notes 48, 60 and
72, and velocities 0.25, 0.55 and 0.85. It measures finite output and headroom,
monotonic touch, energy loss during held notes, and sustain behavior. The
existing BassPluckedRefinement suite remains **149/149**.

| Program | Soft-to-firm range over the three registers | Held decay at 1.3–1.6 s, relative to attack | Material-only normalized shape distance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Harp | 12.59–13.58 dB | −8.39 to −6.92 dB | 0.242493 |
| Koto | 12.84–13.88 dB | −17.55 to −14.25 dB | 0.430294 |
| Banjo | 12.70–13.80 dB | −47.79 to −39.64 dB | 0.476559 |
| Kalimba | 8.83–9.87 dB | −24.50 to −19.57 dB | 0.538561 |

The material control keeps the same complete patch, MIDI pitch, filters,
envelopes and gain, changing only the two material selectors to Standard.
Normalization removes uniform level from the spectral comparison; an output
volume adjustment alone cannot satisfy it. The harmonic projection grid is
extended around the kalimba's nonharmonic upper modes.

The sustain check sends CC64 before note-off. Each body rings longer than with
normal key damping and continues losing energy while pedal-held. Very large
sustain/released ratios for the dry Banjo compare against an almost silent
released tail; they are not presented as a measure of acoustic realism.

## Paired comparison with the previous implementation

There are **36 pairs / 72 full-patch WAV renders** against the c76ad288 baseline:
four programs, three registers and three velocities, held for 2.5 seconds with
1.2 seconds after release. These captures use zero pressure and both ranks.
All candidate streams are finite. The retained JSON records each WAV hash,
executable hashes, candidate core-input hashes, commands and individual metrics.

| Program | Gain-normalized spectral power distance across nine pairs | Early RMS change at firm touch, across three registers |
|---|---:|---:|
| Harp | 0.093–0.212 | +0.27 to +1.22 dB |
| Koto | 0.263–0.604 | −2.69 to −1.68 dB |
| Banjo | 0.310–0.820 | −2.44 to +1.77 dB |
| Kalimba | 1.941–1.995 | +2.84 to +3.75 dB |

These broad spectral distances are L1 distances between normalized power
histograms, not perceptual ratings. Kalimba's large baseline distance also
includes its corrected register; the separate material-only control above
isolates its material change at the same pitch.

Harp's two-second relative aftersound is 4.1–6.0 dB longer at firm touch. Banjo
has a longer finite aftersound than the old near-silent tail, while its held
energy is still down roughly 51–64 dB by 2.0–2.4 seconds. Koto is quieter at
firm touch and up to 7.34 dB quieter at the softest tested touch. Its measured
12.84–13.88 dB touch range is within the existing 8–18 dB requirement, and it
still passes the unchanged bank audibility floor. Levels were therefore left
unchanged rather than recalibrating every short attack to the same RMS.

## Unchanged bank-level gates

The retained [four-program probe](../development/a11/level_probe.cpp) directly
includes and reuses `LibraryAuditTests.cpp::measure()` and its existing
constants. It measures the four-note pressure-ramped phrase and eight-note
full-velocity/full-pressure stress chord at 44.1 kHz.

| Program | Phrase peak, dBFS | Loudest 50 ms RMS, dBFS | Stress peak, dBFS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Harp | −13.852 | −23.067 | −7.230 |
| Koto | −16.778 | −28.690 | −10.620 |
| Banjo | −14.743 | −26.955 | −9.067 |
| Kalimba | −12.808 | −23.330 | −8.614 |

All four pass the existing phrase peak ≤ −1 dBFS, stress peak < 0 dBFS, and
window RMS between −30 and −12 dBFS limits. This subset does not replace the
root's final full-bank audit or native plugin/host validation.

Run the comparison script with `--baseline`, `--candidate`, `--repo`,
`--wav-dir` and `--output` paths. It uses two capture workers and requires NumPy.
The raw WAVs are reproducible scratch artifacts; their hashes and measured
results are retained in source control. The captured candidate is explicitly a
development build, not a released binary or a committed-source provenance claim.

No instrument recordings were fitted, and no blind listening panel was run.
These results establish particular full-patch signal differences, useful touch,
finite decaying bodies, and existing level-gate compliance. They do not establish
that the sound is indistinguishable from a real harp, koto, banjo or kalimba.
