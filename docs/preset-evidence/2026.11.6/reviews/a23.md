# A23 — complete Plucked-family review

## Scope and inventory

Independently reviewed all **17 Plucked programs**, comprising sixteen recipes
in `curated/Plucked.inc` and Stereo Delay Pluck in `curated/Modern.inc`. All are
in the later curated collection. Compared the A15 final-recipe inventory with
the SHA-pinned published 2026.11.4 inventory. Every name, category, index and
host address is preserved. Every program changes **2–49 previous non-volume
controls**, cumulatively including the retained 2026.11.5 work. No program was
added and no production change was warranted by this review.

Every program has active bodies in both ranks. Harp, Koto, Banjo and Kalimba
alone select their new materials; each uses the compatible Model on both ranks.
The other thirteen retain neutral Material selectors. The four new material
recipes keep the played register in both layers. Steel Pluck, Dulcimer Pluck
and Stereo Delay Pluck intentionally retain a short upper octave course.
This is a parameter/recipe observation, not an independent pitch-error test.

## Named roles and complementary evidence

| Programs | Reviewed mechanism and intended distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Guitar I, Nylon Pluck | Rounded nylon excitation, a pluck-position notch, faster upper losses, short contact and a longer lower body. Their bandwidth, resonant size and contact mix differ. |
| Guitar II, Steel Pluck | Brighter steel excitation and longer upper persistence; Steel Pluck additionally uses a receding octave course. |
| Mandolin, Zither | Closely offset steel courses with independent decay. Mandolin has compact attacks without the former continuous tremolo-depth setting; Zither uses longer unequal decays and shallow chorus. |
| Muted Pluck, Dulcimer Pluck | Short palm-muted damping versus a firm pluck with a brief octave course and a longer lower string. **Dulcimer Pluck describes a pluck**, unlike the separate Keys-category hammered Dulcimer. |
| Funky Pluck I/II, Drone Pluck, Pressure Pluck | Deliberate hybrids retain resonant circuit wah, a quiet drone/ring color or pressure-sensitive held circuit energy. A completely acoustic tail would contradict these stated roles. |
| Stereo Delay Pluck | A decaying steel body and short high-passed octave contact feed the explicitly described stereo delay. |
| Harp, Koto, Banjo, Kalimba | Dedicated material spectra replace unrelated accent instruments. Both ranks share the named material, with a short contact yielding to its lower body. |

The last four have existing independent evidence: [A09](a09.md) tests material
shape, loss, bounded live-pitch response and generic-body compatibility;
[A11](a11.md) tests complete-patch touch, held decay, material-dependent spectra,
sustain behavior and unchanged bank-level gates; [A16](a16.md) restores the
Harp/Kalimba rank-reconstruction, audibility and relative-contact-decay checks.
Those suites were reviewed but not redundantly rerun in A23.

## Fresh renders of the other thirteen

Executed **52 complete-Engine captures**: each of the remaining thirteen
programs at MIDI **48 and 72**, velocities **0.25 and 0.85**, pressure zero,
**48 kHz**, a 1.2-second hold and 0.4-second release excerpt. This generated
**83.2 seconds** of current-source signal without saving WAV payloads.

- Every capture is finite, with zero samples beyond full scale.
- Maximum peak is **−22.469 dBFS**.
- Soft-to-firm onset contrast is **7.762–9.542 dB** across the 26 matched pairs.
- The short Muted Pluck reaches effectively silent later windows, as its
  described damping requires. Mandolin clears more rapidly than the longer
  ringing Zither. These are observed capture metrics, not substituted bank
  thresholds or a listening judgment.

## Remaining model limits

The Standard Nylon and Steel generators are shared authored modal profiles;
guitar, mandolin and zither differences combine excitation, courses, size,
filters, touch and decay. They are not fits to particular recorded instruments
and do not implement independent strings coupled through a measured bridge or
soundboard. Their body response is captured at onset through later pitch bends;
the new fixed-Hz correction specifically covers Banjo, Koto and Harp. These
limitations remain distinct from the verified programming changes. No blind
listening assessment or acoustic-indistinguishability result is claimed.
