# A37 — Engine ownership, allocations and complete-Engine choir cost

**No new Engine ownership or callback-allocation blocker was found in the inspected audio source.** Expanded choir histories add **12,800 bytes per Engine**, and low-register choir adds measurable whole-Engine processing work. In the bounded comparison below, median paired `.7/.6` CPU ratios were **1.153 for eight low Ah notes** and **1.167 for eight low Oo notes**. These are shared-host observations with explicit variability, not plugin deadline or platform release qualification.

The audio source is `c5549f7d095a5c5f49a5ad22ec0ccc819245d201`; the baseline is `.6` source `18711f93a39aa49f3d6f9a41784be456224facbe`. The inspected audio/plugin files are also identical in storage-only successor `ee0dabcacb4cdff93f8803844f60348e0ceda6ed`. Its two protected differences are `src/tools/parity_capture.cpp` and `tests/LibraryAuditTests.cpp`. Root owns fresh native qualification of that successor. A37 changed only this review and its evidence directory.

## Ownership and the increased object size

[A11](a11.md), [A07](a07.md), [A21](a21.md) and [A45](a45.md) were reviewed before adding measurements. Their body spectrum, formant, patch-expression and audit-harness experiments were reused. The [source ownership receipt](../verification/a37/ownership-review.json) binds the inspected source and those earlier reports by SHA-256.

`InstrumentBody` already had a fixed 48-slot modal bank. A07 expands its two inline formant-history arrays from 24 to 48 doubles each: **384 additional bytes**. A05 contributes another **16 bytes** of cached noise coefficients. These are inline state, not new per-note heap buffers. The finite Ah/Oo harmonic budget is captured at note-on and clamped to 24–48; pitch changes update the admitted modes without rebuilding a dynamically sized bank. See [InstrumentBody.h](../../../../src/core/InstrumentBody.h).

Each [Engine](../../../../src/core/Engine.h) owns eight voices, two ranks per voice, and both current and previous body state per rank: **32 body objects**. The previous body supports the existing 3 ms reassignment handoff. Copying that body copies inline state; it does not allocate another Engine or another modal buffer. The increased histories also increase that bounded copy's size. Sustained measurements below do not time reassignment handoffs.

| Inline object extent, Linux x86_64 GCC 14.2 | `.6` | `.7` | Increase |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `InstrumentBody` | 6,096 B | 6,496 B | 400 B |
| One eight-voice `Engine` | 491,976 B | 504,776 B | 12,800 B |
| Two processor-owned Engine extents | 983,952 B | 1,009,552 B | 25,600 B |

The fresh probe reproduces A45's body/Engine sizes exactly. The two-Engine row is arithmetic over those object extents; it excludes the rest of the processor, effect allocations and allocator overhead.

The normal plugin entry point, `createPluginFilter()`, returns `new Cs80AudioProcessor()`. Its primary Engine is a value member of that heap-owned processor. Its second Engine is a `unique_ptr` allocated and prepared unconditionally in the processor constructor, before audio callbacks. Sixteen-voice activation resets and reuses that existing Engine. It does not allocate the second Engine on the activation callback. The shared release-order clock is declared before the primary and extension members, so it outlives both during destruction. See [PluginProcessor.h](../../../../src/plugin/PluginProcessor.h) and [PluginProcessor.cpp](../../../../src/plugin/PluginProcessor.cpp).

There is no Engine-sized local or Engine copy in the inspected `processBlock` path. This differs from the automatic Engine in A45's original worker-thread audit. The Engine API remains a large value type; callers outside the normal plugin factory still choose its storage. A45's heap repair and its outstanding native gate are not replaced by this product ownership finding.

## Preparation, onset and rendering are distinct boundaries

`Engine::prepare()` first prepares chorus/BBD/FX buffers at the maximum selectable rate, then reinitializes them at the effective rate. These vector allocations are setup work. `prepareToPlay()` prepares both Engines and sizes the primary stereo, extension stereo and mono external-input vectors, with at least 4,096 frames of scratch capacity. During `processBlock`, `renderSegment` processes bounded chunks when the host supplies a larger block, using those existing buffers.

Note-on captures body configuration and excitation in the fixed state. `Engine::noteOn()` may execute the strike immediately or queue it for the KAS scanner, so counting only the public note-on call would miss deferred onset work. The probe therefore observes **both the note-on API and the first rendered 256-frame block**, then warm rendering and measured rendering separately. Each measured block also calls `setControlFrame` with the unchanged complete factory parameters.

Quality and offline changes are a different callback path: `processBlock` can call `setNonRealtime()`/`setControlFrame()` → `configureInternalRate()`. Reserved capacities avoid new buffer allocations, but this path reinitializes rate-dependent state and effects. In addition, `ReconstructedOscillator::prepare()` acquires the existing shared basis registry's **`std::mutex`**. Its storage is static, but another instance preparing can contend for that mutex. This ownership/preparation implementation is unchanged from `.6`. Zero observed heap allocation does **not** establish a lock-free callback or a real-time deadline. See [Engine.cpp](../../../../src/core/Engine.cpp) and [ReconstructedOscillator.h](../../../../src/core/ReconstructedOscillator.h).

### Observed C++ allocations

The probe replaces ordinary, array, aligned and nothrow C++ allocation operators and records calls, requested bytes and frees only inside named scopes. A positive control observes two deliberate allocations totaling 49 bytes and two frees in each binary. It does not interpose arbitrary C `malloc` calls or instrument the JUCE/host wrapper.

All **88 fresh Engine trials**, including the eight first-touch trials, give the same allocation counts for their version:

| Observed phase, per Engine trial | `.6` | `.7` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Heap Engine construction | 1 allocation, 491,976 B | 1 allocation, 504,776 B |
| Set complete control frame before prepare | 0 allocations / 0 frees | 0 / 0 |
| Initial `prepare(48000)` | 115 allocations, 28,083,112 B | 115 allocations, 28,083,112 B |
| Note-on API; first onset block | 0 allocations / 0 frees in each scope | 0 / 0 in each scope |
| Remaining warmup; timed control-frame/render loop | 0 allocations / 0 frees in each scope | 0 / 0 in each scope |
| Prepared quality/offline transitions and their short renders | 0 allocations / 0 frees | 0 / 0 |
| Destruction | 0 allocations / 116 frees | 0 / 116 |

The preparation byte count is the sum of C++ allocation requests, not measured resident memory. Destruction releases the 115 allocated buffers and the separately allocated Engine. The transition scope selects HQ, returns to the saved factor, enters offline HQ and returns to real-time selection. Its CPU time is excluded from the steady-render comparison. These counts do not qualify every controller sequence, sample rate, host integration or concurrent-instance workload. [Raw observations](../verification/a37/raw.jsonl) retain all phases.

## Bounded paired complete-Engine comparison

Both versions compile the same [probe](../verification/a37/engine_probe.cpp) using **all 233 `src` files, including 182 headers/include files, archived separately from the exact matching source**. Each links its matching existing core archive; both core hashes match A21's earlier binding. The `.6` archive is never combined with `.7` Engine/body headers. [Binding](../verification/a37/binding.json) retains every snapshot file hash, core and executable hashes, commands, compiler, host, affinity and return codes.

Measurement ran **2026-11-24 12:51:03–12:54:01 UTC** on the exposed **AMD EPYC 9V74**, Linux x86_64 kernel **6.18.57**, **GCC 14.2.0**, with `-O3 -DNDEBUG -std=c++20` and the existing core's borrowed-held build setting. Each persistent comparison process was pinned to CPU 0 and used SSE FTZ/DAZ, matching the plugin's denormal-suppression intent. This is the supplied Linux core build, not either native Mac build.

Every trial uses a fresh heap-owned Engine, an unmodified **Choir Pad** or **Dark Choir** parameter vector, source-performance policy, **48 kHz host / 2× / 96 kHz internal**, velocity **0.85**, no pressure or bend, and **256-frame blocks**. The warmup is **96,000 host frames (2 seconds)**. The timed window is **65,536 host frames (1.365333 seconds of audio; 256 blocks)**. Engine construction, preparation, onset, warmup, allocation-buffer setup, output hashing and quality transitions are outside steady timing. Each timed loop includes unchanged `setControlFrame` plus complete `Engine::render`, including the Engine's filters, envelopes, effects and output path; it excludes JUCE processing, host I/O and scheduling.

The four cases use MIDI 36 alone; low stacks **36/38/40/41/43/45/47/48**; or the high control **72/74/76/77/79/81/83/84**. Active voice counts are checked as one or eight. The source factory library reports 523 programs in both binaries, and the complete parameter-vector hashes match across versions for every case.

After one first-touch run per case/version, each case receives five deterministically shuffled **ABBA/BAAB paired blocks**, giving ten timed runs per version: **80 timed trials plus eight first-touch trials**. Both `CLOCK_PROCESS_CPUTIME_ID` and monotonic wall time are recorded. PCM word hashes repeat within every case/version; the high choir control's baseline/current hash also matches. The three low-register cases change as expected. All measured output samples are finite.

| Complete Engine scene | `.6` median ns/host frame | `.7` median ns/host frame | Median paired CPU ratio | Five paired-block range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Choir Pad, low one voice | 2,998 | 3,145 | **1.072** | 0.880–1.144 |
| Choir Pad, low eight voices | 11,874 | 13,316 | **1.153** | 1.024–1.165 |
| Dark Choir, low eight voices | 12,179 | 13,433 | **1.167** | 1.083–1.208 |
| Choir Pad, high eight-voice control | 13,548 | 14,219 | **1.042** | 1.003–1.101 |

The ratio is the median of the five within-block current/baseline mean ratios, so it need not equal the quotient of the displayed marginal medians. [Summary](../verification/a37/summary.json) retains every ratio and timing range. The largest wall/CPU ratio among timed trials is **1.0443**; process CPU timing removes descheduling time but does not remove frequency changes or shared-resource effects. The unchanged high-note control and the wide one-note range show why single-digit overhead precision is unsupported here.

These observations put A11's approximately 1.85× isolated low-Ah body cost in complete-Engine context; they do not reproduce a 2× complete-Engine increase in the stated scenes. They establish neither a universal maximum nor a deadline guarantee. There are no per-callback worst-case measurements, sixteen-voice plugin measurements, live bends, retrigger-handoff timing, higher-rate timing or concurrent-instance contention trials. No further optional timing was run.
