# A06 — fixed body response during standard string bends

Baseline: `18711f93`. Repairs the live-pitch body response of the standard
nylon, steel, bass, clav, and harpsichord models. Production edits are confined
to `fixedFormant()`, `tracksFormants()`, and the corresponding body expressions
inside `stringWeight()`. No piano, tine, reed, material, preset, storage, or
sample-loop changes belong to this assignment.

## Finding and repair

These five models already shaped their captured note-on weights with broad
body responses expressed in Hz. They were excluded from the existing live
formant correction. A ribbon bend therefore moved the oscillating partials
while keeping each partial's original body gain, effectively transposing the
body spectrum with the string.

The same authored body expressions now serve both onset weighting and the
existing bounded correction. The five models join `tracksFormants()`; each
uses exactly 24 modes and fits the existing response arrays. Specialized
banjo, koto, and harp retain their earlier material-specific response.

For onset frequency `f0`, current frequency `f`, and captured stretched mode
ratio `r[h]`, the correction is:

```text
q[h] = F(f * r[h]) / F(f0 * r[h])
S0 = sum(max(dark[h], bright[h]))
S1 = sum(max(dark[h], bright[h]) * q[h])
B = min(1, S0 / S1)
corrected target[h] = original target[h] * q[h] * B
```

Thus the corrected absolute mode sum cannot exceed its captured bound. The
common factor `B` preserves relative harmonic ratios. Existing 16-sample
gain interpolation moves between bounded targets. All new response evaluation
uses rational arithmetic at onset or a pitch-changing control boundary;
there are no new per-sample transcendental calls, allocations, or locks.

The original weights sampled `F(f0 * h)` before stiffness stretching. Keeping
them intact and applying the response ratio at `r[h]` preserves their exact
onset calibration. The resulting per-mode source factor includes
`F(f0 * h) / F(f0 * r[h])`; it remains captured during the bend. This avoids
silently changing steel/harpsichord onset voicing or stiffness in a formant fix.
Pluck location, contact variation, seed, losses, decay control, and partial
onsets likewise remain captured. Returning to `float(f0)` uses the existing
exact-unity bypass.

The physical motivation is qualitative. UNSW's
[Guitar construction](https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/music/guitaracoustics/construction.html)
describes string excitation passing through the bridge to the soundboard and
air cavity, and explains that coupling depends on geometry, frequency, and
materials. The implementation inference is that bending one captured string
should sample a fixed body response. The existing numerical centers, widths,
and gains remain authored voicing choices. This source is not evidence that
these profiles reproduce a particular guitar, bass, clavichord, or harpsichord.

## Independent rendered regression

Added `tests/StringBodyFormantTests.cpp`, using only the public `InstrumentBody`
API. It compares rendered partial amplitudes with independently calculated
fixed-body and transposed-body predictions, rather than a fresh destination
note. A fresh note would change register-dependent damping and could confound
the result.

The analytic oracle includes captured pluck position from the specified seed,
the original two brightness endpoint spectra and interpolation, original-note
losses, decay-control scaling, and the stretched modal frequencies. It uses a
closed-form Hann-window mean of exponential decay. A common normalization or
headroom factor cancels in each partial/fundamental ratio. Contact and partial
onset have settled before the spectral window; the render still includes them.

The main matrix contains 540 rendered gestures:

- Five requested string models.
- Host rates 44.1, 48, and 96 kHz, with 2× internal rate.
- Body sizes 0.65, 1.0, and 1.75.
- Onset MIDI notes 45, 57, and 69.
- Bends of −12, −2, +0.35, and +12 semitones, starting 25 ms after onset
  and completing over 35 ms.
- Harmonics 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, and 9, at their stretched frequencies, measured
  over 170–430 ms. Ratios below 0.003 or modes entering the host fade band
  are omitted; every case must retain at least three measurable partials.

Every measured ratio must be within 0.10 dB of the fixed-body prediction.
For each of the 405 bends of at least two semitones, the fixed-body RMS error
must also be less than one fifth of the transposed-body error. This is a
per-gesture requirement, not only a favorable aggregate.

The unchanged baseline is the negative control: the identical executable
source was compiled with an isolated copy of the three baseline body headers
earlier in the include path. It fits the incorrect transposed-body prediction
and decisively fails the fixed-body regression.

The suite additionally checks return-to-onset spectra, deterministic reuse
after a specialized banjo note, and wide live pitch/brightness motion through
the host Nyquist fade at body-size endpoints 0.5 and 2.5. Returned spectra
differ from a steady original note by at most **0.000321 dB** in normalized
partial amplitude. Both continue the original decay history. All safety
captures remain finite with absolute peak below 0.75.

## Exact unbent PCM preservation

A temporary dual-header comparator instantiated the baseline and current
classes side by side. At 96 kHz internal / 48 kHz host it compared all five
models over this matrix:

- Notes 24, 60, and 96; sizes 0.5, 1.0, and 2.5.
- Velocities 0, 0.35, and 1; initial brightness 0 and 1.
- Decay controls 0.25 and 4; seeds 0 and `0x7812a3`.
- 8,192 samples per case, with brightness changed to the other endpoint
  after sample 4,096 while pitch remained fixed.

**1,080 cases, 8,847,360 sample pairs, zero float mismatches.** This includes
the complete captured onset and a subsequent brightness change. The pitch
was calculated using double note-frequency arithmetic and then converted to
float, matching the note-on frequency's public representation:

```cpp
const float pitch = static_cast<float>(
    440.0 * std::exp2((static_cast<double>(note) - 69.0) / 12.0));
```

An approximate decimal pitch or a frequency calculated with different
float-only arithmetic can differ from that exact onset value. It then enters
the intentional live-pitch response path. Therefore this result is exact
unbent preservation, not a claim that detuned or drifted playback is unchanged.
The comparator is `/tmp/cs80-a06/compare_onset.cpp` in the working session.

## Limits

This is a bounded mechanism correction. The evidence demonstrates a fixed-Hz
body response during live bends, preserves the original excitation/history,
and rejects the former transposition. It does not validate the numerical
body profiles against recordings or establish perceived realism. No listening
panel or full engine phrase audition was performed in this assignment.
Large bends may invoke the common headroom factor and reduce overall level;
that is the existing explicit modal bound, not post-render normalization.
Piano's larger mode bank and reed/tine pickup behavior remain outside A06.
