**Recommendation: retain the two existing recipes; no source correction is justified by this review.** The shared Standard Steel body has a real limit in mechanism specificity. ## What the shared implementation actually does

The following observations are from [InstrumentBody.h](../../../../src/core/InstrumentBody.h), principally `noteOn`, `hitSeconds`, `configureExcitation`, `stringWeight`, `stringLoss` and `makeHarmonicModes`.

- **Spatial weighting:** Standard Steel uses `abs(sin(pi*h*p))/sin(pi*p)`, with `p = 0.135 + pluckOffset` and strike-seeded `pluckOffset` within ±0.006. Piano uses the same sine-magnitude construction, at a different position. The variable name does not uniquely identify the physical excitation.
- **Spectral shape:** Steel multiplies that factor by `h^-1.32`, its authored body response, and `exp(-(h-1)/(3+12*colour+5*velocity))`. A harder strike therefore changes the spectrum, not just the VCA. The full-strike normalization denominator avoids undoing the softer excitation by boosting its fundamental.
- **Contact texture:** Steel adds a separately generated, band-shaped noise transient of amplitude `0.013 + 0.012*velocity`, with a 6 ms decay constant. This is an output texture; it is not a hammer-force signal driving or exchanging energy with the modal bank. The constant is not a measured hammer contact duration.
- **Phase and onset:** Principal Steel and Piano modes both start with small phase perturbations within ±0.075 rad and are read from the rotating imaginary component. Steel also has per-partial onset constants `0.00030 + 0.00075/sqrt(h)` and the common short onset smoothing. There is no explicit triangular initial-displacement solution here. Because the output is an authored audio signal, these phase choices alone do not prove a physical initial-velocity model either.
- **Strings and courses:** Steel uses 24 principal harmonics with stiffness coefficient 0.000012. It has no additional unison-course bank. The preset's two courses are implemented as two octave-separated ranks; their overlapping partials and slight rank detuning can beat. That should not be described as a measured pair of coupled dulcimer strings. Piano's six added, slightly detuned low-order modes are a different authored course design.
- **Decay and body:** Steel's modal loss increases with harmonic order, and the upper rank has a shorter body/VCA decay. Its broad body features remain fixed in Hz through the existing live-frequency correction. Those properties are compatible with the described bright opening settling into a clearer lower body; they do not establish a measured dulcimer soundboard.

## Physical distinction and the inherited wording

Jim Woodhouse's own santoor simulations show missing partials determined by the **striking** position, including every fifth partial at a 2/5 strike point. The simulations separately vary hammer mass and compare a pluck at the same location. Thus the spatial notch pattern cannot by itself distinguish a strike from a pluck. Their hammer force and body radiation are separate parts of the problem. [Woodhouse, §12.2](https://euphonics.org/11-2-hitting-strings-the-piano-and-its-relatives/).

Donald Hall's account identifies finite hammer/string contact, relative mass, stiffness and velocity as relevant to the resulting spectrum; a single assumed brightness slope does not replace that interaction. [Hall, *The hammer and the string*](https://www.speech.kth.se/music/5_lectures/hall/hall.html). Bank and Sujbert likewise model hammer interaction separately from string loss, dispersion and soundboard filtering. This implementation explicitly says it is not their measured model. [Bank and Sujbert, DAFx-02](https://www.dafx.de/papers/DAFX02_Bank_Sujbert_piano_synthesis.pdf).

The concrete limitation is therefore **shared authored steel-string excitation without a separately calibrated hammer/contact model**. The `.6` A17/A42 wording “pluck-spectrum path” is too categorical if read as proof of an incorrect mechanism. This review refines that interpretation; it does not erase the limitation or assert acoustic fidelity. The present Piano body also remains an authored modal approximation rather than a nonlinear contact solver.

## Recommended action

Keep these recipes for the frozen revision and describe the limitation precisely. A Piano switch would also change stiffness, modal count, low-course beating, body features, onset, contact texture, loss laws and register articulation. The measured 11–14 dB settled-level reduction demonstrates that it is not an isolated hammer correction. Adjusting unrelated knobs to compensate would be a new voicing exercise without an established acoustic target.

A future hammered-string specialization would merit evaluation against an explicitly chosen dulcimer/hammer setup, with contact spectrum, register behavior, unison-course beating and body radiation separated in the comparison. It could preserve the two preset identities, but no supported coefficient choice or demonstrated improvement was established here. No new preset, speculative relabeling, general Steel-body alteration, or additional freeze-breaking change is recommended.

No human audition, acoustic-recording match, hammer-solver validation, polyphonic performance study, or native-plugin qualification is claimed by A36.
