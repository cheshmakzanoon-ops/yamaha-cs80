## Recommended bounded fix: preserve body excitation noise across render quality

**Confirmed defect:** the authored contact/breath/bow texture becomes quieter when internal sample rate rises. A normal 48 kHz Standard render uses 96 kHz internally; a circuit-engine offline bounce uses 384 kHz internally. The resulting noise texture loses about 6 dB even though the harmonic body and overall RMS remain essentially unchanged.

The same defect is shared by the additive excitation path in [InstrumentBody.h](../../../../src/core/InstrumentBody.h), particularly `prepare()` and `tick()` around the `airHigh`, `airLow`, and `flutter` updates. `randomBipolar()` supplies a fixed-variance new sample at every internal tick. The two filter coefficients retain their physical 650/5200 Hz settings as the rate rises, so the admitted noise power decreases. There is no reference-rate compensation. [Oversampling.h](../../../../src/core/Oversampling.h) explicitly selects Standard 2x and offline HQ 8x at a 48 kHz host; [Engine.cpp](../../../../src/core/Engine.cpp) passes that internal rate into each body.

This is a source-level explanation backed by output samples, not a listening approval or an instrument-similarity score.

### Full Engine evidence

### Proposed implementation

Compute a scalar during `InstrumentBody::prepare()`, referenced to **96,000 Hz internal**, and multiply only the additive `airLow * (airAmount + hitAmount * hitEnvelope)` term by it. Preserve mode amplitudes, frequencies, phases, note seeds, private RNG consumption, envelopes, timing, controls, program identities, and the 96 kHz internal sound. There is no reason to change preset values or add a parameter.

For exact discrete-filter variance, let `ah = 1-exp(-2*pi*650/Fs)`, `al = 1-exp(-2*pi*5200/Fs)`, `bh = 1-ah`, and `bl = 1-al`. The implemented ordering first updates `airHigh`, then feeds `noise-airHigh` into `airLow`. Its transfer function is:

`H(z) = al*bh*(1-z^-1) / ((1-bl*z^-1)*(1-bh*z^-1))`.

For unit-variance white input, the exact stationary output variance is:

`V(Fs) = (al*bh/(al-ah))^2 * (al^2/(1-bl^2) + ah^2/(1-bh^2) - 2*al*ah/(1-bl*bh))`.

The uniform generator's 1/3 variance cancels in the ratio. Use `gain = sqrt(V(96000)/V(Fs))`, preferably computing coefficients with `expm1`. Gains are 1 at 96 kHz, 1.3934522894 at 192 kHz, 1.95814145463 at 384 kHz, and 2.76115486067 at 768 kHz. This is more precise than a square-root-of-rate approximation, particularly at the lower supported rates. The probe implements the formula and reports its corrected RMS; it does not patch production audio.

The 19 Hz flutter pole has a separate, smaller rate-dependent variance: for its coefficient `af`, its unit-white-input variance is `af/(2-af)`. If normalizing flutter too, give it its own reference scalar rather than scaling the shared RNG input with the air-band scalar. A minimal additive-texture fix can leave that separately measured issue for a follow-up.

Necessary implementation checks: preserve exact 96 kHz internal samples; finite bounded scalars across all legal prepare rates; steady texture across the host/quality matrix; contact energy and headroom across short hammer/pluck/mallet onsets; actual offline-versus-live output texture; unchanged no-body output, block partition behavior, note seed consumption, and identity inventory. These address the concrete correction rather than requiring a general bank rewrite.

## Secondary observation: late sustain pedal cannot catch release

`setSustainPedal(..., true)` only sets a channel bit. Existing release stages are not caught or slowed. A real Engine test plays C4 at 0.8 for 500 ms, releases the key, waits 20 ms, presses CC64, and renders 400 ms. Its samples are **bit-identical** to leaving the pedal up for Stage Keys, Electric Piano I, Reed Piano, Vibraphone, Soft Keys, and Harp. Holding the pedal before key-up gives a markedly longer tail: the first five no-pedal/early-pedal RMS contrasts are −18.87, −18.91, −21.98, −18.95, and −15.79 dB respectively.

This is a playability limitation worth considering for dampered physical-body instruments. It is **not labeled a regression**: current code explicitly documents CC64 as deferred gate release. A re-pedal feature needs a deliberate body-specific damping policy and non-retriggering envelope support; applying it globally would change synth and hardware-study behavior. The rate defect above is the stronger immediate bounded fix.
