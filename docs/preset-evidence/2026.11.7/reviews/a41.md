# A41 — exact material, variant and instrument identity

**PASS for source/recipe identity and the stated signal evidence; no new
freeze-breaking product defect found.** All nine `.6` Materials and all four
retained Variants reach their intended compatible models in the current bank.
They occur in **18 existing programs / 34 active specialized ranks**. The two
remaining ranks in those programs are the intentional circuit consonants in
Robot Voice and Hollow FX 141. No nonzero saved selector is ignored by an
incompatible model, and no program was added or renamed.

This is an instrument-specific accounting, not an acoustic-equivalence
verdict. The material names identify concrete authored spectra, losses and
onsets. They do not establish complete hammer/string, membrane/string,
soundboard, shell, comb or vocal-tract simulations. The known hammered-Steel
limitation remains as qualified by [A36](a36.md); changing those recipes during
this freeze is not supported by the available evidence.

The struck and plucked descriptions therefore have **signal-level support**:
brief contact, velocity-shaped excitation, onset weighting and independently
decaying upper energy. A fitted hammer force or a solved initial string
displacement is a stronger claim. The contact-noise signal is added to the
output; it does not drive a coupled resonator or exchange energy with the
modal bank. The bodies synthesize their signals procedurally and do not load
instrument samples. These observations follow the source, not inference from
a preset name or from one matching frequency ratio.

## Articulation, courses, partial decay and pedal

### Independent amplitude owners

The [Engine onset](../../../../src/core/Engine.cpp) converts the actual sounding
rank frequency to the captured body note after footage, tuning reference,
master tune and rank detune. It captures model, compatible selectors, size,
strike, relative loss and onset. Live pitch subsequently rotates the existing
modes without resetting their phase or rebuilding their bank. Intrinsic color
and body mix remain live; after-brilliance scales pressure passed to the body.
The actual rank HP/LP filters then process the full body before the rank VCA.
At body mix 1, the body replaces that rank's circuit VCA input; saved oscillator
flags do not add an equally loud sustaining oscillator underneath it.

There are two separate decay controls. Each nonsustained mode has its own
positive loss, scaled by the Body decay value: modes below ratio 1.05 use the
weaker `decay^0.35` factor, while higher modes use `decay` itself. The rank ADSR independently
owns overall attack, held contour and release. A longer ADSR or depressed pedal
does not replenish already lost modal energy. Saved envelope times therefore
cannot be reported as measured T60 values or exact audible tail durations.

[BodyArticulation.h](../../../../src/core/BodyArticulation.h) dispatches on the
**base model**, not the Material or Variant. The distinct material profiles
share that base model's register scaling while their recipes and internal
partial laws supply additional differences. At full body mix, decay and
release scales follow `exp2((60 − soundingNote) × slope)`, within the existing
clamps. Bell uses slopes 0.008/0.003; Celesta 0.012/0.008; Tine 0.010/0.007;
Steel 0.009/0.007; Nylon 0.010/0.008; Marimba 0.015/0.008; Vibraphone
0.010/0.007. Thus Kalimba retains Tine-family outer scaling, Koto and Banjo
retain Steel-family scaling, and Xylophone retains Marimba-family scaling.
Organ-base Ah/Oo has neutral outer articulation; its authored vocal partial
entrances and the preset's rank ADSR still shape onset. There is no separate
material-specific damping-pedal law.

### “Courses” and the hammered dulcimer boundary

The material programs' two rank layers must not be equated with physically
coupled strings or resonators. Standard Piano is the model with the explicit
extra six low-order detuned modes: 24–42 principal modes plus six slower
neighbors, smoothly approaching the authored 1.15-cent course detune with
register. Those are prescribed modal doublets, not a solver for energy
transfer among multiple strings and a soundboard. Temple close pairs likewise
remain independently specified modes.

The exact three easily conflated dulcimer identities are:

| Existing program | Named recipe | Body I / II | Material / Variant | Feet | Body decay I / II | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dulcimer (354) | Dulcimer | Standard Steel / Standard Steel, mix 1/0.98 | 0/0 on both ranks | 8′ / 4′ | 1.78 / 0.62 | Hammered-string-style Keys voicing |
| Distant Keys 113 (156) | Dulcimer, distant finish | Standard Steel / Standard Steel, mix 1/0.98 | 0/0 on both ranks | 8′ / 4′ | 1.78 / 0.62 | Covered finish of the same Keys recipe |
| Dulcimer Pluck (402) | Dulcimer Pluck | Standard Steel / Standard Steel, mix 1/0.91 | 0/0 on both ranks | 8′ / 4′ | 1.31 / 0.38 | Separately authored plucked-string recipe |

All three use 24-partial Standard Steel, not Piano's additional low-course
bank or a dedicated hammered-dulcimer Material. Their “courses” are the two
octave-separated ranks. Standard Steel has position-dependent spectral
notches, velocity-dependent high-frequency weighting, stiffness and partial
loss, but no separately calibrated hammer/contact interaction. As [A36](a36.md)
explains, the sine-shaped spatial factor is not itself proof of pluck-only
excitation. “The hammered presets are demonstrably using the wrong pluck
mechanism” would overstate the evidence; “they share a steel-string spectral
approximation without a specific hammer model” is precise.

A36's unchanged actual-patch captures measure 8.453–8.856 dB firm/soft onset
contrast and 26.863–39.164 dB reduction in upper/lower energy ratio from onset
to settled tone. Its temporary Piano-model substitution reduced settled RMS
by 11.117–14.320 dB and changed multiple mechanisms at once. That diagnostic
does not establish an improved dulcimer. Retaining both existing Keys
identities and the separate Dulcimer Pluck is supported; an unvalidated
model replacement or relabeling is not.

### What pedal sustain does

MIDI CC64 defers gate release for the held voice; pedal-up releases it through
the current rank ADSR and the existing panel sustain/release policy. The mode
envelopes continue losing energy throughout pedal hold. This supports the
observed longer held aftersound without asserting a physical damper mechanism.
CC64 is neither a harp's pitch-changing pedals nor a simulated sympathetic
resonance system. The Engine explicitly treats this as a MIDI extension,
separate from the physical CS-80 footswitch convention.

[PluckedMaterialPresetTests](../../../../tests/PluckedMaterialPresetTests.cpp)
tests all four material Plucked recipes with note-off and CC64 and confirms
both longer held sound and continued decay. A15's actual Harp/Koto/Banjo
gesture captures additionally retain the `.6` samples at the tested reference
rate. The bell material and variant suites establish intrinsic decay;
this audit does not turn that into a newly executed per-program pedal matrix
for every struck patch.

## Exact interaction with the four `.7` changes

| `.7` mechanism | This audit's actual population | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noise variance compensation | All 34 specialized ranks have intrinsic contact or vocal air; compensation matters when internal rate differs from 96 kHz. | All nine material profiles and both struck variants retain their modal identities. Different-rate contact/air strength is corrected separately. At the 96-kHz internal reference the gain is exactly 1. |
| Generic fixed-Hz string response | **None** of the 34 specialized ranks newly enters this path. | Banjo/Koto/Harp already used their dedicated fixed-Hz response before `.7` and retain precedence over generic model dispatch. The three Standard Steel dulcimer comparators do enter the generic correction during live pitch motion. |
| Piano principal-partial decay | **None** of the 34 specialized ranks; only base model 1 Piano qualifies elsewhere in the bank. | `.7` adds the prompt-loss term to principal h7+ as well as h1–h6, preventing the old upper-survival discontinuity. The six slower low-order course modes keep their separate loss law. It does not add hammer specificity to Standard Steel or alter the material banks. |
| Low choir harmonic coverage | The twelve Ah/Oo ranks, when captured pitch/size requests more than 24 modes. | Budget is `ceil(clamp(4400/(size × capturedHz), 24, 48))`, with fractional final-mode weighting. No live bank enlargement occurs during a subsequent bend. Existing normalization and host-rate Nyquist remain in force. |

The new choir budget is conditional on **sounding** register. At played MIDI
60 the 16′ Oo foundations in Blade Choir, Midnight Pads 55 and Dark Choir
capture 30 modes while their 8′ partners keep 24. At played MIDI 36, that
16′ foundation is about 32.70 Hz: even 48 harmonics end near 1.57 kHz, below
its size-scaled upper Oo regions. More modes therefore do not imply complete
upper-formant coverage at the lowest notes. The exact nominal 36/48/60/72
counts for every vocal rank are recorded in the A41 recipe data and agree
with [A35](a35.md).

No claim is made that every specialized patch changes at every rate or note.
A17's five complete material-bell pairs are sample-identical at 48 kHz/2×;
A15's 36 Harp/Koto/Banjo gesture pairs are likewise sample-identical at that
rate. A17 separately isolates Agogo contact at HQ: the old HQ/reference RMS
difference of −5.880 dB becomes −0.043 dB across 128 seeds. That is causal
texture evidence, not proof that the dominant pitched full patch changes by
the same amount. A35 distinguishes equal metric rows from true PCM identity.
