# Assignment 11: original-bank finishes and sequence articulation

## Scope and diagnosis

This change owns `src/core/OriginalCircuitLibrary.inc` and
`tests/OriginalArticulationTests.cpp`. It preserves the existing factory names,
ordering, recipe selections and Init early return. The rank-II hardware-card
body-variant swap added by the integration owner is retained.

The following audible defects were reproducible against the 2026.11.4 baseline:

- **Frozen Leads 121** derives from Whistle Lead, whose only sounding
  oscillator is the bypass sine. The previous low-pass and keyboard-brilliance
  adjustments did not reach that signal, and its drift was already zero. Its
  normalized onset was effectively identical to the source.
- **Soft Leads 128** likewise uses a bypass sine from Sine Lead. Most of its
  finish changed inactive colour controls; its release was identical to the
  source, and its small existing attack change provided little distinction.
- The 22 numbered sequence variations set rates from 2 to 9 Hz without adapting
  their inherited dry envelopes to the new note spacing. The unretimed short
  accent faded too early in slow patterns or occupied too much of fast ones.

## Changes

All 14 finish definitions now have a dedicated amplitude/touch contour for an
audible rank that consists only of the bypass sine. Each changes useful onset,
release or dynamic behavior without adding another oscillator, changing footage,
adding a body model, or inventing a pitch interval. Approximate mid-velocity
level compensation prevents the touch adjustment from being merely a gain
increase. Their descriptions now explain their amplitude behavior rather than
claiming filter colour that the signal cannot receive.

For ordinary filtered oscillators, the finish also shapes the opening filter
envelope crest. Zero-depth filter envelopes remain zero. Body-model voicings
retain their defining model, modal intervals and existing restrained colour
handling; this extra circuit-envelope crest change does not double-colour them.
Inactive ranks no longer receive these finish edits.

Sequence articulation now uses the engine's actual gate: 58% of each period,
or 45% in random mode. Onsets stay concise; the main body and separate short
accent have different decay scaling. Releases are bounded by the available
gap. The ringing main rank of bell-family sequences keeps its existing decay
and release, and delay/LFO/effect timing is unchanged. The sequence rates,
modes and octave patterns remain those of the existing programs.

## Render verification

The new test renders the actual `Engine` at 48 kHz through the circuit path.
It passed **63 checks, zero failures** against the current source. The same
test, built against the isolated 2026.11.4 headers and core library, produced
**63 checks, eight failures**. Baseline failures specifically identify both
sine onsets, the Soft release, and all five tested sequence timing cases.

The complete sine patches were played at MIDI note 60, velocity 0.70, held for
0.6 seconds and then released. Onset RMS is measured over 5–25 ms relative to
the 0.3–0.5 second held level; release RMS is measured 40–90 ms after note-off
relative to the preceding held level. These relative measures avoid treating
master-volume recalibration as an articulation improvement.

| Program | Baseline onset ratio | Revised onset ratio | Baseline release ratio | Revised release ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Frozen Leads 121 | 0.430181 | 0.226466 | 0.111369 | 0.205432 |
| Soft Leads 128 | 0.893841 | 0.739601 | 0.089101 | 0.136119 |

An explicit negative control moves the low-pass, filter envelope and keyboard
brilliance drastically. Its rendered waveform remains unchanged for these
bypass-sine voices, confirming that those inactive controls cannot satisfy the
new audible-contour test. The source waveform selection and register are also
checked unchanged.

For sequence timing, a **diagnostic common sine carrier** isolates rank II's
authored amplitude contour. The real arpeggiator, circuit VCA, rate, random-mode
gate and envelopes remain active; effects are disabled and one held note uses
one octave. This is an articulation measurement, not a claim about the complete
program's spectrum. The reported ratio is the last 65–95% of the first gate
relative to 15–35% of that gate. A separate control copies the source recipe's
fixed envelope while retaining the new program's rate.

| Program | Gate duration | Baseline late/early ratio | Revised late/early ratio | Fixed-time control |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Amber Sequences 157 | 145.0 ms | 0.024248 | 0.130968 | 0.022016 |
| Distant Sequences 159 | 232.0 ms | 0.005793 | 0.039397 | 0.005793 |
| Neon Sequences 160 | 56.3 ms | 0.138761 | 0.078781 | 0.145915 |
| Distant Sequences 173 | 210.9 ms | 0.006495 | 0.044090 | 0.006495 |

The slow accents now retain useful body through their longer gates, while fast
accents recede sooner. All tested dry accents clear after key release. A
separate **complete-patch** render of Slow Sequences 166 verifies that its bell
resonance and echo continue after the key is released. All captures are finite,
audible and below full scale.
