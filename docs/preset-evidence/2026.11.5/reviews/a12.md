# A12 — factory mallet and bell programming

## Changes

Reprogrammed all 22 existing voices authored in `src/core/curated/Bells.inc`.
The 23rd curated Bells program, Ambient Bells in Modern.inc, inherits the
revised common mallet touch level. No preset was added, renamed, reordered,
or removed. Existing master-level calibration files were left to root's
complete-bank pass.

The common mallet setup now has a low fixed VCA floor, making initial touch
responsible for most of the played level. Individual electronic ring bells,
Gong, Metallic Keys, Agogo and Ring Mallet receive separately chosen floors
and contours, preserving their intended character. The prior normalization
correction also preserves upper-mode excitation at gentle strikes.

- **Xylophone:** selects the new third-tuned wooden-bar material on both
  ranks. The supporting rank now shares the played register instead of
  supplying an unrelated octave. A short high-pass mallet accent sits above
  the fundamental/third body. The primary bar decay retains its tuned upper
  mode through the opening, then clears repeated notes.
- **Steel Drum:** selects the shell profile with tuned octave and twelfth.
  Full body mixes remove the old residual pulse, and the fixed 157 Hz ring
  carrier is disabled. A wider, less resonant main filter preserves the shell
  modes, and the longer primary contour lets the resonance outlast contact.
- **Marimba:** a fuller wooden aftersound and quieter upper knock distinguish
  its warm fourth-tuned body from the new shorter third-tuned Xylophone.
- **Vibraphone:** the complete metal body has more held aftersound; key release
  now damps the bar promptly. The published fourth-harmonic strike and 5.2 Hz
  tremolo are retained. Existing global sustain controls remain available.
- **Tubular Bells, Chimes, Clock Bells:** removed fixed ring carriers that
  created sidebands unrelated to the played notes; corrected their technique
  tags and descriptions accordingly. Named electronic ring programs keep
  their carriers.
- **Glockenspiel and Celeste Bells:** full metal bodies and modest amplifier
  aftersound expose independently fading modes. Celeste now has short key
  damping. Glockenspiel's brief accent uses the same material family.
- **Gong, Temple Bell and Dark Bell:** differentiated slower bloom, low
  aftersound and restrained upper clang. Temple Bell's full body allows a
  small amplifier sustain while its own modes continue to decay.
- **Music Box, Crystal Bell, Bell Choir, Metallic Keys, Agogo and Ring Mallet:**
  separately adjusted contact, held decay and/or release to give the named
  strike time to establish before its upper components disappear.
- **Bell Pad:** a softer opening, quieter sustained upper residue and more
  expressive pressure support its intentionally sustained bell-and-sine bed.

Nonzero amplifier sustain is used only with a complete decaying body in the
natural mallet voices. Mixed electronic voices retain zero sustain except
for the explicitly sustained Bell Pad. This prevents oscillator residue
from leaving an unintended held tone.

## Audio evidence

Built a private Release core/capture against the integrated Engine, body
normalization, articulation and variant implementation. All measurements
below come from actual stereo Engine rendering at 48 kHz. No output samples
were normalized, limited or edited. Master-gain calibration is not included
in these development measurements; the reported ratios are relative.

New `tests/MalletPresetRefinementTests.cpp`: **136 checks, zero failures**.
It renders all 23 curated Bells voices for soft and firm strikes; three
registers for material spectra and incorrect-material controls; and held
versus released Vibraphone notes. All renders were finite and below full
scale. The soft-note audibility gate passed throughout.

The intended mode exceeds the wrong-material control by at least 75 dB in
these windows. Xylophone's third also exceeds the fourth by more than 12 dB.
Steel Drum's octave remains above −18 dB relative to its fundamental. These
are narrow component measurements, not overall improvement scores.

At the same sounding pitch, the 650–850 ms wood aftersound is −17.20 dB
relative to onset for Marimba and −30.90 dB for Xylophone, establishing their
different decay roles. Vibraphone key release at 700 ms produces −72.44 dB
less RMS than the still-held reference in the 1.05–1.25 s window. The old
recipe produced only −2.94 dB in that same release comparison.

The existing `PresetPrecisionTests.cpp` also passes: **285 checks, zero
failures**, including its Vibraphone fourth-mode-versus-octave regression
and 230.4 seconds of rendered circuit audio.

The 100-check subset that does not require newly appended variant fields
was also compiled against the exact frozen 2026.11.4 source and library.
It fails 24 intended behavior gates: all 23 soft/firm contrast gates and
Vibraphone's key damping. The three-register material checks instead use
explicit wrong-material controls inside the current Engine, avoiding an
ABI mismatch between the 200-field and 202-field parameter arrays.

## Limits and integration

The new xylophone and steelpan profiles follow the material relationships
and sources documented in A10. Their additional ratios and loss constants,
as well as these preset values, remain authored voicings. No test here
establishes perceptual indistinguishability from recorded acoustic
instruments.

Root owns CMake registration (`cs80_mallet_preset_refinement_tests`, linked
to `cs80_core`, timeout 120), full-bank gain calibration and native plugin
validation. Files released by this assignment: Bells.inc, the new test, and
this report. No commits or remote changes were made by this agent.
