# A28 — Keyboard polyphony, register and pedal edges

## Outcome and scope

No blocking defect was found in this bounded independent review. The current
probe passes **102 checks with zero failures**; the same probe linked against
the exact frozen 2026.11.4 bank/engine reports **two failures out of 102**,
both for Toy Piano's compressed low/high polyphonic touch. These checks
supplement the existing single-note keyboard and body-articulation suites;
those suites were not rerun wholesale.

Six unchanged program identities are covered: Stage Keys, Tine Piano, Reed
Piano, Harpsichord I, Clavichord I and Toy Piano. This is rendered software
behavior, not a listening panel or a claim of equivalence to an acoustic
recording. The extreme chords are engineering probes rather than recommended
acoustic playing ranges.

## Conditions and causal comparisons

A private C++ harness uses the complete `Engine` with each unmodified factory
patch. The rate is 48 kHz, pressure is zero, MIDI channel is 1, and each capture
lasts 2.4 seconds. The eight-note intervals are `{0,4,7,12,16,19,24,28}` from
roots 24 and 72, giving played MIDI spans **24–52** and **72–100**. All eight
notes begin together. The selected recipes retain their own footage, filters,
body mixtures, circuit contributions, controllers and effects. Toy Piano's
actual bodies therefore sound above those played-key numbers.

For each of the twelve program/register cases, four complete renders use:

- A soft 0.20-velocity chord, keys held until 1.6 seconds.
- A firm 0.90-velocity chord, keys held until 1.6 seconds.
- The firm chord with key-up at 0.7 seconds and no pedal.
- The firm chord with CC64 down at 0.35 seconds, key-up at 0.7 seconds, and
  CC64 up at 1.6 seconds.

Every render splits at the same event boundaries, so block partition changes
cannot masquerade as a pedal effect. A separate idle render for each recipe
measures any stationary output/effect floor. Thus each version executes
**54 captures, 129.6 seconds of stereo audio, and 6,220,800 stereo frames**.

The displayed touch contrast is `20 log10(firm RMS / soft RMS)` over 0–0.7
seconds. Early key damping compares the released and still-held versions at
the identical 0.95–1.2-second window; this removes the body's natural decay
from that comparison. Pedal-up damping compares 2.0–2.35-second RMS against
the preceding pedal-held 1.3–1.55-second window. The acceptance bound also
allows the independently measured idle floor in quadrature.

The complete CC64-held audio is compared sample by sample against the direct
held-key capture, including the common release starting at 1.6 seconds. This
checks that key-up does not start damping prematurely under sustain and that
pedal-up applies damping exactly once across all eight sounding voices.

All **54 current captures are finite and below full scale**. The largest
peak is **−9.321 dBFS**, leaving substantial headroom in this tested gesture.
Across the ten touch-sensitive conditions, soft/firm contrast ranges from
**10.134 to 12.216 dB**. Harpsichord I deliberately remains almost velocity
independent at **0.091–0.092 dB**; stronger hammer-like response would be the
wrong contract for that authored quill registration.

All twelve pedal/direct-hold differences are **exactly zero samples**, before
and after pedal-up. Early key damping is at least **35.639 dB** relative to the
matched still-held chord. The later post-pedal window is at least **61.314 dB**
below its preceding held body. No stuck pedal tail or prematurely damped held
body appears in these cases. CC64 here is the plugin's performance function;
this result does not imply that a physical harpsichord has a piano sustain
pedal.

All six idle captures are digitally zero in both builds. The logs print
`−300 dBFS` because their display clamps zero before taking a logarithm; that
number is not a measured physical noise-floor claim. Other chorus-bearing
programs are outside this specific selection, so their stationary BBD noise
must still be treated separately by the existing keyboard regression.

Every compared patch has master volume **1.000** in both builds. These
soft/firm ratios are therefore not a result of different calibration trims.
The baseline passes the broad pedal, damping and headroom safeguards; its two
failures are Toy Piano's **5.055/5.661 dB** contrast against the probe's
predeclared 6 dB minimum. The more substantial contrast improvements in Stage,
Reed and Clavichord are visible in the table without claiming that every
baseline case must fail.
