# A17 — Pads and Polysynth recipe refinement

## Scope and implementation

All **30 existing Pads.inc recipes and 24 existing Polysynth.inc recipes** received individually programmed performance changes. Names, categories, tags and order match the preceding source files exactly. This assignment adds no presets and does not change Init Circuit.

The previous pad fixed VCA levels often contributed more than the entire initial-touch route. Soft and firm notes therefore differed by only roughly 3 dB. Each revised recipe balances its fixed level and played level separately for the main body and upper layer. The firm chord remains close to its previous electrical level, while quiet gestures can recede. The changes preserve each voice's waveform, harmonic footage, deliberate ring/PWM/tremolo identity, and independent pressure routes.

Polysynth recipes receive their own comparable touch balances and deliberately shorter comping releases. Long Synthwave Chords and Dark Poly endings remain longer than the compact Square/Bright/Saw Pulse voices. Poly Pluck's held main decay is shortened from 4.2 to 3.4 seconds to keep a held chord recognizably plucked. Pressure Pad and Poly Swell retain pressure-led performance: their quiet beds remain available, with greater room for the held swell. Sine Pad, Sine Poly and Low Drone no longer add free oscillator drift; the deliberately detuned programs retain it.

### Choir voices

Choir Pad and Dark Choir now use the vocal variants supplied by [A16](a16.md). The first uses two open ah bodies with slightly different sizes; the second uses covered oo bodies in its existing 16-foot/8-foot registration. Both use full body mixes and wide, low-resonance panel filters so the authored vocal resonances remain audible instead of being replaced by keyboard-tracking filter bands. Unequal entrances, restrained chorus, controlled rank balance and pressure effort give a choral gesture without added free pitch wobble.

These are synthesized vowel profiles, not recordings or a measured singer reconstruction. The 24-mode source has finite harmonic bandwidth; the extreme low extension cannot represent every high vocal formant. A16 supplies the direct-body spectral, bending and standard-output compatibility evidence. This assignment verifies their integrated recipe dynamics and levels.

## Rendered verification

`tests/PadPolyRefinementTests.cpp` renders the actual Engine with the complete factory settings. It covers all 54 owned recipes at MIDI 60, plus 18 register cases at MIDI 48 and 72, paired pressure renders and five comping-release comparisons. There is no post-render level normalization.

- New suite: **311 checks, zero failures**; local measured runtime **74.6 seconds**.
- The same behavioral assertions against frozen 2026.11.4: **311 checks, 72 failures**. These expose the intended touch, pressure, held-decay and release differences.
- Existing PresetVoicingTests: **284/284 checks passed**, including slow pad bloom and held short articulations.
- Existing PresetPrecisionTests: **285 checks, zero failures**.
- Source identity comparison: all **54 names, categories, tags and ordering** unchanged.
- Scoped `git diff --check`: passed.

Register `cs80_pad_poly_refinement_tests`, linked to `cs80_core`, with a 180-second timeout. Parent integration owns the final bank calibration and native platform/host qualification.

### Response measurements

Soft and firm gestures use velocity **0.20 and 0.90**. Sustained levels use 1.5–2.25 seconds; the three held chord-hit voices use their 20–200 ms opening. The 29 effort-driven pads improve from **2.63–4.00 dB** to **7.50–8.86 dB** of contrast at MIDI 60. The 23 effort-driven polysynth voices improve from **5.28–10.12 dB** to **9.40–11.84 dB**.

Pressure Pad and Poly Swell are deliberately excluded from those effort-driven ranges. At velocity 0.55, their full-pressure versus zero-pressure contrasts are:

| Program | Previous | Revised |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pressure Pad | 15.83 dB | 19.87 dB |
| Poly Swell | 13.03 dB | 17.36 dB |

Released-versus-held comparisons use identical notes and timeline, measuring 120–170 ms after key-up. Spectral evolution in a held voice therefore cannot be mistaken for damping.

| Program | Previous | Revised |
|---|---:|---:|
| PWM Poly | -19.46 dB | -30.23 dB |
| Bright Poly | -30.43 dB | -41.80 dB |
| Square Poly | -19.74 dB | -29.56 dB |
| Saw Pulse Poly | -19.67 dB | -29.99 dB |
| Contour Poly | -19.31 dB | -28.01 dB |

Poly Pluck now falls **34.97 dB** from its opening to the 1.8–2.3-second held window. Poly Stab and Poly Brass Stab remain fully decaying hits.

### Level and headroom checks

All 54 recipes also ran through the existing `LibraryAuditTests::measure` phrase and eight-note stress renderer, with the existing trims. Its loudest 50 ms phrase window spans **−23.75 to −17.45 dBFS**, within the unchanged bank gate. The largest phrase peak is **−4.68 dBFS** and largest eight-note stress peak is **−2.93 dBFS**. The two final Poly Pluck/Poly Swell changes were measured again after the full pass.

Choir Pad's phrase window is **−23.58 dBFS** with **−8.85 dBFS** stress peak; Dark Choir is **−21.65 dBFS** with **−5.44 dBFS** stress peak. Their pure vocal-body outputs are quieter than the former filtered oscillators; this is reported separately from their improved touch response. The remaining pads retain approximately their previous firm-note level.

## Limits

The numerical results demonstrate stronger controllable expression, clearer released chord transitions, preserved held articulation, audible integrated vocal bodies and headroom. They do not establish subjective perfection or indistinguishability from acoustic recordings. Final recalibration may change absolute factory volume; the response and release comparisons are relative measurements and are exercised again by the shipping build's test suite.
