# A44 — Longer environmental and mechanical FX phrases

## Result

**102 checks passed, zero analysis failures.** Thirteen actual Engine renders
cover seven existing programs, **17,280,000 stereo frames at 48 kHz**, or six
minutes of audio. Every sample is finite and below full scale. The highest
single-note peak is **−14.871012 dBFS**, in Clockwork. No production correction
is indicated by this bounded review.

The review confirms a useful distinction: Wind and Rain have changing random
contours, Surf and Thunder have separately retriggered one-shot contours, and
Clockwork, Helicopter and Siren retain their deliberately periodic behavior.
It does **not** establish equivalence to a weather recording, physical machine,
listening preference or native plugin execution.

## Why another bounded probe was useful

A18 already checks the revised environmental mechanisms, including twelve
seconds of Wind, 2.5 seconds of Rain continuity, short one-shot contours, and
two seconds of Clockwork. A33 checks a matched single-note excerpt from every
program. This follow-up targets the remaining longer-window integration risk:
continued random motion after the initial entrance, repeated weather events
without resetting the Engine, a longer run of mechanical ticks, and sustained
pressure changes in intentionally periodic effects.

The private diagnostic renders factory settings at played MIDI 60 and velocity
0.9. There is no output normalization. Causal controls alter only the recorded
modern-LFO fields; the analysis verifies every one of their 202 binary32
parameter values against the exact committed inventory and those explicit
changes. It also verifies all ordinary factory vectors and existing host
indexes, frame counts, sample bounds and independently recomputed peak/RMS.

## Wind and Rain after the opening envelope

Wind uses independently seeded smooth-random filter and amplitude routes at
0.19 and 0.31 Hz. Rain combines its 13.7 Hz fine intensity contour with 0.23 Hz
filter and 3.7 Hz amplitude motion. The Engine's three modulation generators
have separate deterministic random streams. Their targets update at the
specified rates; successive target values do not form a repeating waveform.
Ordinary note-on does not reseed these global generators.

For each program, a 36-second actual factory render is paired with the same
patch with the three modern LFO depths set to zero. The following values
measure the difference between corresponding **25 ms output-RMS windows from
12 to 36 seconds**. They describe output motion, including the filter's effect
on noise energy; they are not claims of a purely multiplicative gain stage.

| Observation | Wind | Rain |
|---|---:|---:|
| Standard deviation of moving-minus-flat window level | 1.407392 dB | 0.371900 dB |
| Range of that window-level difference | −1.892531 to +4.046535 dB | −1.151867 to +1.039589 dB |
| RMS difference between windows 1/0.7 seconds apart | 0.822791 dB | 0.504373 dB |

The 0.7 Hz lag tests Wind's former periodic pumping rate. The same lag is only
a common diagnostic interval for Rain; it is not asserted to be Rain's prior
modulation rate.

A separate positive control enables only Wind's amplitude LFO, retaining its
depth but assigning a **0.7 Hz triangle**. Its lagged mismatch is only
**0.007575 dB**, with correlation **0.999947**, versus **0.822791 dB** and
correlation **0.834274** in the actual Wind trajectory. The same whole-patch
measurement therefore distinguishes a deliberately repeating control from the
revised gust contour. This bounded result does not claim the complete signal
contains no periodic component or that every possible random trajectory has
been examined.

Rain also receives a separate paired control that removes **only LFO1's fine
amplitude depth**, leaving its slower filter and amplitude trajectories intact.
Over **0.2–36 seconds**, the largest adjacent **2 ms** window-level change is
**0.060951 dB**; the 99th percentile is **0.043266 dB**. The longer excerpt
exposes a greater maximum than A18's shorter excerpt without introducing the
large sample-and-hold steps that A18's causal comparison identified. This is a
windowed rendered-level measurement, not a proof of absence of every audible
click under every gesture.

### Stereo scope

These remain intentionally narrow, centered synthesized textures. In the
same late-held interval, Wind has L/R correlation **0.999590** and side-to-mid
RMS **−33.481 dB**; Rain has correlation **0.999505** and side-to-mid RMS
**−31.461 dB**. These values describe the subtle authored rank panning. They
should not be advertised as spacious environmental recordings or a measured
acoustic scene.

## Surf and Thunder retrigger as events

Each complete patch is rendered for 40 seconds, with note-ons at **0 and 16
seconds** and ordinary note-offs at **12 and 28 seconds**. The same Engine
continues throughout; there is no reset, forced mute or all-notes-off shortcut
between events.

| Program | First event | Second event |
|---|---|---|
| Surf | Maximum 50 ms crest window begins at 1.60 s; late held output is 59.566 dB below that crest | Crest again begins at 1.60 s after its own note; late held output is 60.269 dB below its crest |
| Thunder | Upper-band energy share falls from 37.166% in the crack to 6.073% in the roll; late held output is 60.257 dB below its crest | Crack/roll shares are 38.150% and 5.401%; late held output is 60.315 dB below its crest |

Late-held energy uses 8.5–11.5 seconds after each note-on, before its note-off.
The broad 1.5 kHz upper-band split follows the production FX test, but its
analysis filter runs continuously through the entire recording. The second
note is not given a fresh analysis-filter state. Both renders finish with
**zero active voices** after their normal release tails.

The measured behavior supports one breaking wave or thunder event per key,
with a fresh contour on the next key. It does not establish a physical model
of breaking water, lightning propagation or environmental reverberation.

## Intentional periodicity remains audible and controllable

Helicopter and Siren receive separate twelve-second factory renders at zero
and full pressure. Positive crossings of the actual SUB readout are measured
after the first two seconds. Readout sampling is every 256 host frames
(5.333 ms), so short-cycle timing scatter includes that quantization and is
**not** a physical oscillator-jitter measurement.

| Existing program | Measured cycles without pressure | Measured cycles at full pressure | Final Engine rate readout at full pressure |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Helicopter | 11.504306 Hz | 13.614522 Hz | 13.611340 Hz |
| Siren | 0.700062 Hz | 0.808688 Hz | 0.808804 Hz |

The Helicopter signal also contains the intended strong periodic amplitude
motion. After low-pass filtering its stereo-mean power at 30 Hz and sampling
that envelope at 200 Hz, a sinusoidal projection at the measured SUB
fundamental accounts for **96.623%** of envelope variance without pressure
and **98.614%** at full pressure. This analysis suppresses audio-carrier
aliasing before reducing the sample rate. Those fractions measure periodic
power motion; they are not acoustic-similarity scores.

Clockwork is held for twelve seconds and released for four. Sixty complete
late-held ticks, spanning the interval from one to eleven seconds, show:

- Within-tick body level **17.295–17.854 dB below** each tick's onset.
- Between-tick gaps **55.182–67.220 dB below** onset.
- Zero active voices at the end of the ordinary release.

Its six-per-second ticking remains distinct from a permanently sustained tone
with square amplitude gating. The slower 3 Hz SUB filter alternation retains
the intended tick-tock color. A request to remove robotic sound from the
weather presets does not justify removing the deliberate rhythm from a
program named Clockwork, Helicopter or Siren.

compile attempted range iteration on the indexed `ParamValues` type. The
snapshot was expanded to the exact commit's full `src` tree, and the private

explanations are retained. These were not native plugin failures or modified
product code; the final private compile, all thirteen renders and the 102
analysis checks completed successfully.

The test covers one deterministic noise trajectory per case, one played note
and the specified pressure gestures. It does not establish subjective
preference, an exact acoustic identity, every register/chord/random state, or
native host compatibility. No production source, preset table, shared build,
workflow, commit or remote ref was changed by A44.
