# A38 — Independent organ registration and played-phrase review

## Outcome and scope

No release blocker was found in the reviewed organ behavior. The 17 existing
ordinary organ programs retain their named stop registrations and deliberately
velocity-independent playing response. The revised bank gives them straighter
pitch and more individually timed keying. Tremolo LFO Organ also has a stronger,
correctly isolated pressure contribution from its upper stop.

This review covers the fourteen entries in `curated/Organ.inc`, Tremolo LFO
Organ, and the original Tone I: Organ 1 and Tone II: Organ 2 studies. Choir
variants are outside this assignment. No preset, production source, shared
build, workflow, calibration table or host identity was changed here.

Source reviewed: `6094bce18c8b19da804c2fbc9a00e31969a7f579`, factory revision
**2026.11.5**, against the frozen **2026.11.4** bank and core. The focused
[probe](../verification/a38-organ-probe.cpp) and
[review checker](../verification/a38-review-organ.py) produced
**212 passed review checks, zero failures**. These are local review diagnostics;
they do not replace native CI or qualify an installation bundle.

## Source and inventory findings

All seventeen retain the previous footage, body model, body mix, intrinsic
brightness, decay and size settings. The ordinary organ material remains
Standard. Both ranks keep zero initial-touch brilliance and level routes.
The two original tone studies correctly inherit the revised curated recipe,
with the Tone II study's ranks exchanged as intended.

The principal changes are audible performance details rather than replacement
registrations:

- Straight stops now use zero dispersion and drift. Combo Organ also removes
  its small fixed detune. Named chorus, SUB vibrato, SUB tremulant and modern
  LFO tremolo remain separate, intentional routes.
- The main and upper ranks have individual onset times. For example, Flute
  Organ 8+4 changes from 10/16 ms to 18/9 ms, while Church Organ changes from
  55/38 ms to 60/22 ms. This makes the lower foundation speak more gradually
  than the upper registration in those voices.
- Dry releases are shorter. Church Organ changes from 260/170 ms to
  135/85 ms; Pipe Organ changes from 220/130 ms to 120/65 ms. These are
  authored envelope controls, not measured acoustic decay constants.
- Percussive Organ retains its sustaining 8-foot lower rank and separate
  zero-sustain 4-foot percussion. The upper decay becomes 680 ms instead of
  820 ms, and both release controls become 22 ms. The existing dedicated
  percussion test already checks the octave attack and its held decay, so
  that complete suite was not repeated here.
- Tremolo LFO Organ raises only the upper rank's after-touch level from 0.14
  to 0.22. Its lower stop has no after-touch destination.

The Engine mixes each optional harmonic body into the rank **before** its
existing VCA, so the revised amplitude envelopes affect the audible modeled
body. The normal organ model fixes its strike and phase seed, and the new
body-articulation layer returns neutral multipliers for organ. Consequently,
struck or bowed velocity-dependent timing cannot accidentally enter these
ordinary organ stops. The circuit-only Combo Organ remains circuit-only.

The exact old/new control differences for every reviewed program are retained
in [a38-organ-review.json](../verification/a38-organ-review.json).

## New diagnostic: overlapping chords, repeated keying and pressure

Existing family tests already cover single-note velocity invariance, sustained
level, key damping and selected registers. This review adds a bounded phrase
that exercises eight voices during a chord overlap, followed by a repeated
chord without resetting the Engine.

At 48 kHz, the phrase plays `[48,55,60,64]` at 0 seconds and
`[50,57,62,65]` at 0.5 seconds. The first chord releases at 0.54 seconds;
the second releases at 0.98 seconds. The first chord returns at 1.1 seconds
and releases at 1.6 seconds. Rendering continues to 2.2 seconds. The three
chord attacks use velocities 0.2/0.9/0.2; a matched control exchanges them
for 0.9/0.2/0.9. A separate matched control applies channel pressure 1 at
0.25 and 1.3 seconds, returning it to zero at 0.75 and 1.5 seconds.

All **17 programs have exact sample-for-sample equality when the velocities
are exchanged**. The sixteen programs without a pressure route also have
exact equality between pressure and no-pressure phrases. Tremolo LFO Organ
responds to pressure as intended. All three chord segments remain audible,
and every sample is finite and within full scale. The largest phrase peak is
**−6.819 dBFS**, in Combo Organ.

The final 1.8–2.0 second tail is compared with the preceding 1.3–1.5 second
held chord. The least attenuated final tail is **−53.832 dB relative to the
held chord**. All final tails are below −81.450 dBFS. The named chorus and
tremolo paths remain enabled throughout.

| Existing program | Previous tail relative to held chord | Revised tail relative to held chord |
|---|---:|---:|
| Flute Organ 8+4 | −62.634 dB | −66.729 dB |
| Percussive Organ | −74.278 dB | −77.690 dB |
| Church Organ | −55.618 dB | −60.712 dB |
| Chapel Organ | −55.751 dB | −60.085 dB |
| Pipe Organ | −59.623 dB | −60.814 dB |

Sixteen of seventeen tails have greater attenuation in this particular window.
Theatre Organ changes slightly from −54.639 to −54.577 dB, with its intentional
tremulant/stereo tail still present. This is not evidence that every acoustic
release is universally better or that more damping is always desirable.

## Registration and pressure isolation

Five contrasting registrations were checked at MIDI 60 using the rank-mix
endpoints as causal diagnostics. The expected frequency lines were specified
independently of the stored footage controls. Stereo Hann projections over
0.5–1.1 seconds compared each expected line with the other tested footage
lines at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 and 4 times the keyboard pitch.

| Existing preset | Lower / upper nominal footage | Sounding frequency ratios |
|---|---|---|
| Flute Organ 8+4 | 8′ / 4′ | 1 / 2 |
| Flute Organ 16+8 | 16′ / 8′ | 0.5 / 1 |
| Chapel Organ | 8′ / 2′ | 1 / 4 |
| Church Organ | 16′ / 4′ | 0.5 / 2 |
| Fifth Organ | 8′ / 5⅓′ | 1 / 1.5 |

All ten expected fundamental lines are present and dominate the other tested
footage lines. The smallest margin is **4.704 dB**, in Church Organ's lower
rank; the upper harmonics there are intentionally substantial. These
projections establish register identity, not a complete frequency-estimation
or tuning-parity result.

For Tremolo LFO Organ, separate full-patch and isolated-rank renders at
velocity 0.7 compare pressure 0 and 1:

| Pressure response | Previous | Revised |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full-patch held level increase | 0.283 dB | 0.618 dB |
| Full-patch octave/root spectral-ratio change | 0.973 dB | 1.642 dB |
| Isolated upper-stop held level increase | 2.821 dB | 4.118 dB |
| Isolated lower-stop change | Exact unchanged PCM | Exact unchanged PCM |

This confirms that pressure brings the intended 4-foot stop forward rather
than adding an unrelated lower-rank swell. The ratios use the same complete
signal path and master setting within each pair; analysis does not normalize
or alter rendered audio.

## Limits

The organ body remains an authored sparse harmonic registration. This review
does not establish a specific pipe organ, free-reed organ or tonewheel
instrument's acoustic equivalence. There is no measured organ recording or
blind listening reference in this diagnostic. Church Organ and Pipe Organ
provide dry registrations with room for external reverberation; their names
do not make the current dry output a captured hall response.

The changes reviewed here refine the existing registrations' stability,
keying and selected expression. They do not introduce a new per-pipe
physical model, full drawbar instrument or a new preset. Native plugin
validation and downloadable-artifact qualification remain the parent
release task's responsibility.
