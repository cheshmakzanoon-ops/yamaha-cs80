# A16 — stationary vocal resonators for the existing choir programs

## Delivered scope

Added two **saved body variants**, `choirAh = 3` and `choirOo = 4`, compatible
only with the existing Organ body (`model = 15`). Their host labels are **Choir
ah** and **Choir oo**. No model ID, model-selector range, preset name, preset
count or published core-parameter metadata was changed. Only the two newly
introduced, unpublished variant controls extend their maximum from 2 to 4.
The parent owns host integration and A17 owns the two existing choir recipes.

The main files are `src/core/InstrumentBody.h`,
`src/core/InstrumentBodyVariants.h`, the two new maxima in `Params.h`, and
`tests/VocalBodyTests.cpp`.

## Sound mechanism and acoustic basis

A complete harmonic source with a declining glottal spectral tilt excites
four broad, fixed-Hz vocal-tract regions. The first two regions distinguish
open **ah** (authored centers 760 and 1180 Hz) from covered **oo** (330 and
850 Hz). The existing body-size control scales their geometry. Higher broad
regions shape the singing color. These numbers, bandwidths and weights are
voicing choices, not measurements of a singer or a universal phonetic table.

The source/filter distinction follows UNSW's research explanation and measured
examples:

- [Voice acoustics: source, filter, vowels and resonance tuning](https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/voice.html).
- [Measurements of glottal activity, tract resonances and output sound](https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/glottis-vocal-tract-voice.html).
- [Terminology and the distinction between resonances and spectral peaks](https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/formant.html).

The harmonic pitch moves through these stationary tract responses during
slides, ribbon motion and vibrato. The response is recalculated on the existing
control boundary and bounded with a common gain. The existing interpolated
mode gains avoid abrupt gain changes. Unlike the standard organ registration,
the vocal source has a soft, effort-dependent onset, gradual partial emergence,
restrained turbulent breath, and routed-pressure spectral tilt. Its small
nonperiodic color motion does not change pitch or oscillator phase.

The implementation reuses the existing 24-mode fixed-formant response arrays
and amplitude-sum bound. It adds no object storage, allocations, locks, or
transcendentals to the per-sample mode loop. All source harmonics retain the
existing host-rate Nyquist fade.

The spectral comparison covers three singing registers. Fixed-tract comparisons
cover octave slides in both directions, small pitch excursions, three sizes,
two vowels and all three sample rates. Safety cases include MIDI 0–127,
velocity endpoints, body-size endpoints, upward motion, pressure, nonfinite
pitch handling and suppression of harmonics above the host Nyquist frequency.
All rendered safety cases remain below the 0.76 peak guard.

A deliberately deficient **standard-organ plus two-pole low-pass** control

of the old complete Choir Pad recipe. The same test compiled against the exact
pre-A16 header, with variant constants passed as integers 3/4, maps them to

number of harmonic checks differs because the old sparse registration has
fewer measurable partials. This rejects absent vowel identity and absent voiced
effort/pressure behavior.

The existing `InstrumentBodyTests` still passes **2,077 checks / zero failures**
over **41,062,350 samples**. The existing mallet-variant suite passes **1,132
checks / zero failures**; its incompatible-pair sweep correctly omits the newly
valid Organ/choir combinations.

## Limits

These are synthesized vowel colors, not recorded choirs, articulated words,
consonant models, individual singers, or a coupled larynx/tract simulation.
The 24-harmonic budget resolves the first two vowel regions well through the
ordinary choir range; very low notes have limited upper-region coverage, and
high pitches sparsely sample the lower resonances. Real singers may retune
resonances with register. The tests establish stationary tract behavior,
expressive source changes, output bounds and compatibility, not perceptual
indistinguishability from a real choir.
