# A10 — xylophone and steelpan excitation variants

## Implemented behavior

`InstrumentBody` now accepts the saved, append-only material choice as the
last optional argument of `noteOn`:

```cpp
noteOn(model, note, velocity, brightness, decay, seed,
       size = 1.0f, selectedVariant = standard);
```

The public body model range remains 0–19. `standard` remains zero. Xylophone
is compatible only with Marimba (18); Steelpan is compatible only with
Vibraphone (17). Invalid or incompatible combinations use Standard. A new
note resets the material choice, so a reused voice cannot retain a previous
variant. Selection uses saved numeric parameters, never preset names.

| Material | Intended audible distinction | Excitation and decay |
|---|---|---|
| Xylophone bar | Strong tuned third above the fundamental; sparse, nonharmonic upper bar modes | Short wooden contact, touch-dependent upper energy, fast upper-mode losses, compact pitched decay |
| Steelpan shell | Fundamental, octave, twelfth and two-octave tuned modes; weaker higher shell resonances | Broader contact, increasing shell brightness with strike, longer fundamental and separately decaying higher modes |

Both variants use eight modes in the existing fixed-capacity bank. All
additional `pow`, `exp` and `log2` work occurs at note initialization; the
sample loop is unchanged. The existing output-rate Nyquist fade and control
interpolation apply to both materials. There are no new allocations, locks,
sample-loop transcendental calls, clipping stages or output normalization.

The full-strike absolute-weight bound from A07 is retained. New variants
compute that bound with full-strike contact bandwidth, while applying the
actual strike to their sounding partial weights. Gentle strikes therefore
do not receive a reciprocal fundamental boost as their upper modes recede.

## Reference basis and limits

[Yamaha's manufacturer guide](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/marimba/structure/structure003.html)
describes its xylophone bars as tuned to the fundamental and third partial,
and marimba bars to the fundamental, fourth and tenth. The guide also notes
that other manufacturers may use different xylophone tuning practices. The
implemented variant deliberately represents the fundamental/third approach.

[Rossing, Hansen and Hampton, JASA 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10955647/)
report steelpan tuning of octave modes and, when possible, a twelfth or
two-octave mode. These relationships motivate the distinct shell profile.

The additional noninteger mode ratios, excitation weights, contact curves
and decay constants are original voicing choices. They are not extracted
measurements of a particular instrument. The tests establish the intended
audible relationships and numerical behavior; they do not establish
indistinguishability from physical instruments or recordings. Final preset
sound also depends on the rank envelope, filter, oscillator blend and effects.

## Validation

All tests below were freshly compiled against the modified header with
GCC, C++20, `-O2 -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic`.

| Validation | Result |
|---|---:|
| New `InstrumentBodyVariantTests.cpp` | 1,133 checks, 0 failures |
| Existing `InstrumentBodyTests.cpp` | 2,077 checks, 0 failures; 41,062,350 generated samples |
| Existing `StruckBodyDynamicsTests.cpp` | 3,439 checks, 0 failures |
| Independent standard-path comparison against the post-A07, pre-A10 header | 540 matching rendered sample-bit hashes |

The new suite measures actual audio with Hann-windowed arbitrary-frequency
DFTs and independent local spectral-peak searches. It covers:

- Distinct bar/shell resonances at MIDI 48, 60 and 72, at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.
- Preserved played fundamental, plus the intended third for Xylophone and
  octave/twelfth for Steelpan.
- Higher partials decaying faster than the fundamental, with a shorter wood
  envelope than the shell envelope.
- Soft versus firm strikes across MIDI 36, 60 and 84 and dark, intermediate
  and bright settings: no fundamental normalization boost, no early-RMS
  inversion, and at least 6 dB of upper/fundamental contrast change.
- Fundamental and material-overtone consistency between host sample rates.
- Exact sample equality of omitted, explicit Standard, invalid and
  incompatible variants for every body model, including live pitch, color
  and pressure input. Reusing a variant voice then striking Standard also
  matches a fresh Standard voice exactly.
- Finite, audible excitation with peaks below 0.80 at low/high keyboard,
  strike, brightness and decay endpoints, with 1× and 2× internal rates.

Representative measurements at 48 kHz, MIDI 60, velocity 0.85 and brightness
0.65: Steelpan's octave is −9.02 dB and its twelfth −14.62 dB relative to its
fundamental in the early window. The third/fundamental ratio falls by
21.40 dB for Xylophone and 9.16 dB for Steelpan between the early
(35–270 ms) and late (550–950 ms) windows. These are behavioral measurements,
not scores of realism.

The independent comparison compiled the same capture driver against the
saved pre-A10 header and the modified header. It covered all 20 models,
three rates, MIDI 36/60/84 and velocities 0.2/0.7/1.0, including live pitch
and color variation. The two complete 540-line sample-bit-hash outputs were
identical under `cmp`. The pre-A10 header already contained A07's dynamics
correction; this comparison specifically isolates the material-variant work.

Reproduce the committed new suite from the repository root:

```sh
g++ -std=c++20 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Isrc \
  tests/InstrumentBodyVariantTests.cpp -o /tmp/cs80-body-variants
/tmp/cs80-body-variants
```

Root integration owns the Engine call, saved parameter wiring, factory
recipe selections and CMake registration. This assignment creates no preset
and changes no existing model number or host parameter range.
