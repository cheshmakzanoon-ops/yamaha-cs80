# A19 — Existing modern preset refinement

## Scope and outcome

Reprogrammed all 18 existing recipes in `src/core/curated/Modern.inc`. Added `tests/ModernPresetRefinementTests.cpp` as a rendered behavior regression suite. No presets were added, renamed, reordered or recategorized; tags and descriptions remain unchanged. The source check also confirms every original modern arpeggiator rate, direction and octave span, and every explicit modern LFO rate, destination, depth and waveform selection remains unchanged.

The final modern suite passes **123 checks with zero failures**. Restoring only the old Modern recipes on the same current engine and family helpers fails **31 of those 123 checks**. The exact production level measurement passes all 18 programs, and `OriginalArticulationTests` passes all 63 checks after the changes. No master-level calibration files were modified by A19.

Keys, plucked and bell categories use the first 350 ms; sustained and sequence categories use 250–1500 ms of a 1.6 s held note. This is a matched playing-response measure, not a loudness-normalized timbre preference score. MIDI note 60 is used except Drive Bass at note 48.

## Struck-body aftersound, echoes and spectral motion

The tine, steel and ambient bell main ranks use 100% decaying body models. Their small outer-envelope sustain pedestals therefore retain each body's intrinsic loss law instead of leaving a static circuit oscillator underneath. The second ranks retain compact, decaying attack envelopes. At a held C4, the final 4.5–5.0 s window remains 28.887 dB below the tine onset, 33.386 dB below the steel-string onset and 24.048 dB below the ambient bell onset. These tails continue to decay.

An otherwise identical zero-pedestal control is respectively **9.846, 22.208 and 7.554 dB quieter** in that late window. This causal control demonstrates the aftersound was previously choked by the outer envelope; it does not establish equality to a recorded acoustic instrument.

After a 160 ms note, the 420–620 ms echo window in Delay Brass, Space Lead and Stereo Delay Pluck is respectively **51.027, 73.796 and 48.828 dB above an insert-dry control**. The point is clear separation of the dry release and audible echoes, rather than a claim that the ratio alone measures musical quality. The one-hertz tine pan is also tested in separate stereo channels and crosses both sides of the center.

For sweep, PWM and phaser programs, the suite removes only the named LFO depth or first phaser wet path. It compares the time-varying spectral ratio against that frozen control over 3–9.5 s. Broad normalized first-difference energy measures the filter/phaser changes; PWM requires the Hann-windowed even/odd harmonic ratio, because duty-cycle changes can be large while overall spectral brightness barely moves. This choice avoids treating amplitude movement as proof of spectral movement.

| Program | Spectral ratio maximum/minimum relative to frozen control | Log-ratio standard deviation |
|---|---:|---:|
| LFO Sweep Pad | 1.402 | 0.087 |
| PWM LFO Strings | 13.702 | 0.670 |
| Phaser Strings | 1.212 | 0.061 |
| Phased Polysynth | 1.413 | 0.095 |

These named movements were already real in the preceding modern recipes; their tests establish that the touch and articulation work preserved them. The actual modern waveform enum in `src/core/ModulationLfo.h` is 0 sine, 1 triangle, 2 saw up, 3 saw down, 4 square, 5 sample-and-hold, 6 smooth random. All existing Modern selections agree with those definitions.

The tremolo organ retains a measured quarter-second amplitude recurrence, versus a half-period negative comparison. Its 75–105 ms post-release window is **−57.956 dB** relative to its final held interval, compared with **−46.732 dB** for the old Modern recipe on the same current engine. This gives cleaner key damping without imposing piano-like velocity response on the organ.

## Reproduction and limits

Register `tests/ModernPresetRefinementTests.cpp` as **`cs80_modern_preset_refinement_tests`**, linked to `cs80_core`, with a 180-second timeout. The final private run took **52.700 seconds** while another complete same-engine comparison was running concurrently. The test is deterministic and uses no network, plugin host or external recording.

A19 compiled the current `FactoryPresets.cpp` into a private object and linked it before the freshly built `build-preset/libcs80_core.a`; this ensures current recipe data while reusing the shared current engine. The isolated old-recipe comparison compiled the same current factory and family helpers with only the Modern include redirected to the frozen baseline. The previous-release comparison used the frozen baseline headers and `baseline-build/libcs80_core.a`, avoiding any 200-versus-202 parameter-layout mix. Private logs and executables were kept under `/tmp/cs80-a19` during the work.

The figures above are objective rendered software evidence. They establish materially stronger playing response, clearer dry articulation, preserved named effects and longer decaying struck aftersound. No claim of exact hardware parity, exact recorded-instrument identity, or blinded listener preference is made. Final all-platform binaries, master-trim calibration and the repository-wide release gates are owned by the root integration pass.
